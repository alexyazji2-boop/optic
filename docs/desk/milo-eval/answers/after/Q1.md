The business looks superb on every number Optic has, but I can't tell you the price is cheap. That turns on whether $MU's profits stay near today's level, and this data can't settle it.

**The business.** In the fiscal year to August 2025, from the company's statements, revenue was **$37.38bn**, up 48.9%, with a 26.2% operating margin [[src research]]. That is a year old. The quarter to May 2026 alone had revenue of $41.46bn and net income of $28.24bn [[src company]], more than three times fiscal 2025's whole-year profit of $8.54bn.

**The price.** At the $953.19 close [[src quote]] the trailing P/E (price over the last 12 months of earnings) is **12.82** and the forward P/E (price over expected earnings) is **4.62**. That is the kind of number that makes you check the decimal point. My read is that a multiple that low means the market doubts the earnings will last: forward EPS of $206.33 is about 2.8 times trailing EPS of $74.33 ($206.33 / $74.33), so 4.62 only holds if profits keep climbing.

**What argues against it.** The statements show a full swing: net income of $8.69bn in fiscal 2022, a **$5.83bn loss** in fiscal 2023, then $0.78bn and $8.54bn [[src company]]. Even in a good year, fiscal 2025 capex was $15.86bn, 42.4% of revenue, leaving free cash flow of only **$1.67bn** and $5.64bn of net debt [[src research]]. Headlines tie today's 3.6% drop partly to Chinese chip competition, a risk no multiple captures [[src news]].

**Not available.** Valuation against Micron's own history and peers isn't computed here, so "cheap against its past" is a gap, not a finding. The next report, due 23 December, is the next test of the earnings that forward multiple leans on.

**Bottom line.** Strong business, unresolved price. If profits fall back toward the earlier years, today's multiple would look very different. That's the data and what it can't tell you, not a call on your money.
