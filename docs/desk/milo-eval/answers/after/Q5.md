Tomorrow's CPI can move tech stocks. Nothing on this page says which way, and I'd rather tell you that plainly than guess.

**What CPI is.** The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks how fast everyday prices are rising. It comes out tomorrow, Wednesday, at 8:30 AM ET, before the market opens [[src market]]. Last time, prices rose 0.4% in the month, and over the year they were up 3.71% [[src research]]. Why it matters to you: the Fed (the US central bank) watches this number when it decides on interest rates.

**Why rates touch tech.** Think of a savings account. The more it pays, the less a profit ten years away is worth to you today. Tech stocks lean on far-off profits, so they tend to feel rate worries first. The 10-year Treasury yield (what the government pays to borrow for ten years) was 5.24% on Oct 9 [[src research]]. Why it matters: a hot CPI could make traders expect high rates for longer, and that can weigh on tech. A cool one could do the opposite. Those are scenarios, not forecasts.

**Where tech stands.** The Technology sector fund (XLK) closed Tuesday almost flat, down 0.04%, but it is down 2.83% over the week and up 6.51% over the month [[src market]]. Nvidia rose 0.97% on the day but is down 4.48% on the week. Apple rose 0.09% and Microsoft 0.43%. Why it matters: stocks that have climbed over a month are the ones a rate scare can dent. That is my read, and nothing here says it will happen.

**What I can't tell you.** Optic doesn't have analysts' forecasts for CPI, so I can't say what counts as "hot" or "cool". A number matters mostly against what people expected. I also can't see which tech stocks you own, and Optic has no brokerage link. Prices here are delayed. The VIX (a gauge of how big a swing traders expect) is 15.3, which is calm, so the market isn't braced for much. It's the one report where the stock market behaves like a student waiting on grades.

**What comes next.** Producer prices (PPI) come out Thursday, and the Fed decides on rates on Oct 28 [[src research]]. Those can move things too.

My honest read: the signals don't give an edge on direction, and the market is going sideways for now [[src market]].
