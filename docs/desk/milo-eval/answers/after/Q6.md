Partly. The number the Desk leans on is real, but it shows the biggest names lagging the average stock, which is not quite the same as "the softness sat in the biggest names."

**What the Desk is resting on.** Over the five sessions to Oct 13, the equal-weight S&P 500 beat the cap-weighted one by **0.6 percentage points**, from Yahoo Finance daily bars [[src research]]. That is the fund holding every company equally minus the fund tilted to the largest. Positive means the average stock did better than the index's largest names. The Desk reads that as softness in the biggest names [[src desk_section]].

**Where it holds up.** It fits a WSJ headline from the same day that mentions a pullback in AI stocks [[src research]]. It is also consistent with a flat day: the S&P 500 fund (SPY) closed 0.01% lower while energy rose and health care fell, which is not a story about the biggest names alone.

**Where it is thinner than it sounds.** First, a gap between two funds says one beat the other, not that either fell. Both could have risen, and the data on this page has no absolute five-session figures for either, so "softness" is not shown. Second, equal-weight and cap-weighted funds also differ in sector mix, so a sector move can open a gap without the biggest names doing anything. Third, the breadth line is 5 of 11 sector funds higher, which is a one-day split, not a read on the largest companies. The Desk's own headline names a bank and health care as that day's weak spots, which is a different window and a different group. Five sessions is short, and 0.6 points is a small margin to hang a story on.

My read is that the conclusion is plausible, not demonstrated. The one live check, energy at +1.80%, is unchanged since publication, so nothing has moved that would alter it. The Desk is analysis, not a source, and this section is only as good as that one comparison.

**What would settle it.** The five-session returns of the largest individual holdings, and of the two funds in absolute terms.
