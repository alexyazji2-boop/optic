**Because the data has two different "what analysts expected" numbers, and JPMorgan beats one and misses the other.** So the Desk doesn't pick a side.

**The numbers.** For the quarter reported on Oct 13, JPMorgan's earnings per share (EPS, its profit divided by its number of shares) came in at **$5.75** [[src research]] (from the Optic Desk, using Yahoo Finance data). The consensus (the average of analyst forecasts) is $5.715, inside a range of $5.65 to $5.78. Against that, $5.75 is a small beat: $0.035 over, about 0.6%. But the feed also carries a second estimate, $5.94, and against that the result is a **miss of 3.24%**. A rare case where the honest scorecard says "depends which scorecard."

**Why that matters.** "Beat" and "miss" only mean better or worse than expected. When the expectation itself is unclear, the label is a guess dressed up as a fact. The data doesn't say which of the two figures is the better one, so the Desk leaves it open, and I'd do the same.

**What the stock did.** JPMorgan's shares fell 0.86% that day. The data shows the move, not the reason. Revenue against its forecast isn't in the free data either, so there is no second test to break the tie.

**For somebody holding it.** Don't treat a "beat" or "miss" headline on this quarter as settled. One bank's quarter is a small piece of evidence, and the next big test for bank stocks is tomorrow's inflation report at 8:30 AM ET.
