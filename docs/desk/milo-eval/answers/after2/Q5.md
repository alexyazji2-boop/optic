**Short version: CPI can move tech stocks a lot, and nothing in the data here says which way.** CPI (the consumer price index, a measure of how fast everyday prices are rising) comes out Wednesday at 8:30 AM ET, an hour before the stock market opens [[src research]]. It's the market's monthly pop quiz, and the answer key isn't on screen.

**Why tech cares.** Many tech stocks are priced on profits expected years from now. If inflation runs hot, traders bet the Fed (the US central bank) keeps interest rates higher for longer. Higher rates make far-off profits worth less today, a bit like a better savings rate making it less appealing to wait for a payoff. So a hotter number tends to hurt tech and a cooler one tends to help, though that isn't a rule. Optic's own note says rate expectations usually reset within seconds of the release [[src market]].

**What I can see.** The last reading was a 0.4% rise in August, and prices are up **3.71%** over the year to August, against the Fed's 2% aim [[src research]]. Oil is at $95.28, which can push the headline number up. Traders tend to focus on core CPI, which leaves out food and energy. The 10-year Treasury yield (what the US government pays to borrow for ten years) is **5.217%**, and it's a rate tech investors watch closely.

**What I can't see.** Two things. First, what economists expect. A CPI number only moves markets against that forecast, and forecasts aren't in the free data, so I can't say what would count as a surprise. Second, which tech stocks you own, because this page doesn't list them. For tech as a group, the sector fund (XLK) is down 2.83% this week but up 6.51% over the month [[src market]], so it goes in a little bruised after a good run. Those are Tuesday's closing figures.

**Why it matters to a holder.** The first move on Wednesday may be about CPI alone. PPI (prices companies pay at the factory gate) lands Thursday at 8:30 AM ET [[src research]], so there are two inflation reads in two days, and a one-day jump or drop can fade by the next. This is not advice, just what the data can and can't tell you.
