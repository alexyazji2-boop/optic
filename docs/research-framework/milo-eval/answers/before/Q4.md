A small miss, and today's move says little about how the market judged it.

**How it went**

JPM reported **$5.75** a share on 13 October against $5.94 expected, a 3.2% miss (Yahoo Finance earnings history) [[src research]]. It's the second miss in the last eight quarters, after beats of 5.9% and 7.8% in the two before it, and the eight-quarter average surprise is still +6.2% [[src company]]. Revenue against its estimate, credit losses, net interest income (what the bank earns on loans less what it pays on deposits) and guidance text aren't in the data, and those usually decide how a bank's report is read. One headline says JPM raised its full-year net interest income outlook despite a revenue miss, but I only have its title [[src news]].

**What the move says**

JPM is at $333.68 (delayed about 15 minutes), up **0.68%** today [[src quote]]. That's about as close to a shrug as a stock gets, though one day of shrugging isn't a thesis, and it opened at $327.17, below yesterday's $331.43 close, so it climbed back from a weak start. Optic hasn't loaded the report-day reaction on 13 October. Over 21 sessions JPM is down 5.34% against 4.58% for the financials sector fund, so the slide is mostly the group's [[src sector_confirm]]. A price move describes the stock's reaction, not how good the quarter was.

**What argues against it**

The chart is in a dip rather than a verdict: price sits below its 20-, 50- and 100-day averages but 3.6% above the 200-day at $322.22 [[src technicals]]. Optic's score is leaning bearish at -10.3 with low conviction [[src verdict]], but it's an untested summary that leans on headlines mostly covering a report already out.

**Bottom line**

My read: a modest EPS miss, no punishment so far today, and too little data to call the quarter good or bad. The credit-loss and net interest income figures in the results filing (an 8-K filed 13 October) [[src research]], or a close below the $323.54 support [[src technicals]], would change it. The next report is 14 January 2027. That's the data and its limits, not a call on your money.
