**The business**

Over the four quarters to July 2026, $NVDA brought in **$302.97bn** of revenue [[src research]] at a net margin of **63.7%**, and the latest quarter grew **105.9%** on the same quarter a year earlier (company statements via Yahoo Finance). In the fiscal year to January 2026 it made **$96.68bn** of free cash flow (44.8% of revenue) on capex of just 2.8% of revenue, spent $40.09bn on buybacks and cut its diluted share count by 1.17%. Debt of $11.04bn is roughly matched by cash (net cash of -$0.43bn), so the balance sheet isn't the worry. A 63.7% net margin is the kind of figure that makes you check the decimal point twice. What the data doesn't cover is competitive position or customer concentration.

**The price**

At $222.36 (regular session, delayed about 15 minutes) the trailing P/E is **28.11** and the forward P/E is **13.97** [[src research]]. That gap means analysts expect earnings per share to roughly double (28.11 / 13.97 is about 2.0), so the cheap-looking forward number holds only if the forecast does. Whether 28x is rich for this company needs its own multiple history, which Optic doesn't compute here.

**What argues against it**

Margins this high are the likeliest thing to fade, and a forecast-based multiple looks cheap right up until the forecast moves. It has beaten estimates in 8 of the last 8 quarters, by **5.8%** on average [[src company]], which makes a beat the base case rather than news, so the 17 November report will turn on guidance. A beta of 2.216 [[src quote]] means the shares have historically swung about twice as hard as the market, whatever the business does.

**Bottom line**

My read: leaning positive on the business, no clear edge on the price. A fall from the **60.4%** operating margin of the fiscal year to January 2026 [[src research]], weak guidance on 17 November, or a multiple history showing 28x is high for $NVDA would change it. That's what the data supports, not a call on what to do with your money.
