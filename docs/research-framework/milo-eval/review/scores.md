## Errors by case

### Q1 NVDA long-term
- **A:** "Debt of $11.04bn is roughly matched by cash (net cash of -$0.43bn)". The brief says NVDA holds net cash. This cash figure is too narrow (it probably leaves out marketable securities), so the balance sheet reads as slight net debt.
- **A:** "Whether 28x is rich for this company needs its own multiple history, which Optic doesn't compute here." This is an honest limit, so it is scored as a usefulness cost, not an accuracy error. The P/E sits low in its five-year range, so the deciding half of the question is left open. A also gives no returns-vs-cost-of-capital evidence and no explicit bull case.
- **B:** "a price that screens cheap against its own history and peers". The answer never shows a peer comparison, so "and peers" is unsupported.
- **B:** "Optic's DCF base case is $723.51 a share (+225%)". The figure is sourced and caveated, but leading with a precise model value comes close to a price target. The support-level table under "Where to reassess" drifts into the trade for a long-term question. Net cash and the flat share count are not mentioned.
- Checked and correct in B: +225%; the levels 8.6%, 15.8% and 24.8% below; the 1.8% FCF yield fits A's $96.68bn of FCF; the 47-point margin range.

### Q2 XOM swing setup
- **A:** "an earnings release in the window caps an ordinary swing at B [[src framework]]". The brief says no earnings date reached either version's swing read, so the framework's B cannot rest on this rule. The reason for the grade is misattributed.
- **A:** "the 20-session range (19.04) added to the breakout level". 176.41 + 19.04 = 195.45, not $194.69. The target actually uses 175.65, which conflicts with the 176.41 that A calls the breakout level everywhere else.
- **A and B:** "RSI is 74.9" (B: 74.8). The brief has RSI at about 71. The gap probably comes from intraday vs closing data, but neither answer explains it.
- **A:** the position-sizing example ("19 shares and a $98.72 planned loss") is labelled an illustration, but it edges toward allocation.
- Checked and correct in A: 1.5 x 3.46 ATR = 5.19; reward-to-risk 3.45, and the 2.82-3.77 range across the entry zone; 13 days at about 0.4 ATR a day; the dates of PPI, monthly expiry, VIX expiry and FOMC.
- **B:** "sits at its 52-week high of $177.355". The brief's 52-week high is $176.41, which is the breakout level. B never names 176.41 or the 175.65 resistance, so its levels miss the structure of the setup. Its "$176.88 breakout support", 0.04% below spot, is not a meaningful level.
- **B:** "If a pullback to the $169.93 to $169.14 area holds on a close, the trend read stands; a close back under $174.89 says the breakout failed". A pullback to 169 has to pass through the close under 174.89 that B calls a failure, so the guidance contradicts itself.
- **B:** gives no entry, no confirmation rule and no reward-to-risk. It never mentions relative strength, although the brief says it is strong against the S&P 500 over three months.

### Q3 AAPL earnings
- **A:** "covers 23 other days too". 14 October to 6 November is 23 days counting the report day, so "other" overcounts by one. This is trivial.
- **A:** the P/E at the top of its five-year range is not mentioned. There is no separate prediction for guidance or for the reaction, and the answer does not say how the 4.4% was isolated.
- **B:** "guidance above the 2.91 next-quarter consensus" and "the guide against 2.91". Apple guides revenue and margins, not EPS, so this compares unlike things.
- **B:** "about 1.8x the 2.46% average report-day move ... though Optic's like-for-like test calls it fair (0.95)". These are two opposite readings of the same priced move, and B does not reconcile them.
- **B:** "guidance is likely to support estimates, since last quarter's operating margin was 2.6 points above a year earlier". This is a thin inference, since a past margin gain does not predict a guide. It is labelled low confidence, which helps.
- Checked and correct in B: the 4.42% band ($328.42-$358.80) is centred on $343.61; 2 November is a Monday; the 5.54% straddle and the 23-day span.

### Q4 JPM earnings
- **A:** "JPM reported $5.75 a share on 13 October against $5.94 expected, a 3.2% miss". The feed's calendar consensus is $5.715, and against that figure it beat. A gives no basis and does not raise the conflict.
- **A:** "today's move says little about how the market judged it" and later "no punishment so far today". The report came out on 13 October. That session gapped up about 1.7% and closed about -0.9%, a reversal A misses because it treats today as the reaction.
- **A:** gives nothing specific to watch next quarter beyond the date.
- **B:** "Adjusted EPS of $5.75 missed the $5.94 consensus by 3.2%". B has the same dual-consensus gap: against the feed's $5.715 it is a beat, so the verdict depends on the source. B does at least state the basis.
- Checked and correct in B: the +1.7% gap, the -0.86% close and the -2.51% from the open all match the brief and each other; +13% on $5.07 a year earlier.
