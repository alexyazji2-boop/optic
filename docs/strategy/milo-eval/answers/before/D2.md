Net GEX is **+$272.8m per 1%** with spot 8.95% above the $206.06 flip [[src gex]]. Under the model's dealer assumption (long the calls customers sold, short the puts they bought, which is an assumption and not observed inventory) hedging leans against moves, so damped is a fair word. A pin is a different claim. It wants a large positive-gamma strike close to spot that is about to expire, and the chain doesn't offer that:

- The biggest strike is $245 (+$78.2m, 8.3% above spot), which is also the call wall. The nearest sizable ones are $230 (+$37.2m, 1.6% above) and $240 (+$35.2m).
- The nearest listed expiry, Oct 19, carries only 2.1% of total gamma and Nov 6 another 5.7%. About 92% sits in Nov 20 and Dec 18 [[src greeks]]. Long-dated gamma slows drift, but it doesn't hold a stock to a strike on a given day.
- The strike with the most raw gamma by open interest is $220 (2.8% below spot), but that figure is unsigned, so it says where hedging sensitivity is concentrated, not which way it pushes.

Below the market the structure turns lumpy. The $215, $205 and $200 strikes carry negative net GEX (-$13.7m, -$13.9m, -$20.7m), which is where the model has hedging adding to a fall, though the flip itself is $20 away. Realised vol contracting (10-day 22.1% against 60-day 36.1%) is consistent with the damping read, though it doesn't prove it [[src technicals]].

The weak joint is the sign. Call volume at $230 and $240 was heavy (15.3k and 16.4k contracts), and the flow data infers side from volume and open interest because there's no tape. If customers were writing those calls rather than buying them, the dealer positioning flips for those strikes and so does the read.
