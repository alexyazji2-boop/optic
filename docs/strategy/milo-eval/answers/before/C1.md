Neither one is wrong. They're measuring different things, so the useful question is which timescale you care about.

The chart read is about structure: where price sits relative to its moving averages (the smoothed average closing price over a set number of days). $AAPL is at **$340.97**, above the 20-, 50- and 200-day averages, with the three stacked in bullish order, and RSI (a 0 to 100 gauge of how strong recent gains have been) is **60.2** [[src technicals]]. That's a slow, backward-looking picture of a trend that is intact.

MACD is about momentum, meaning the pace of the trend rather than its direction. It's the gap between a fast and a slow moving average, compared against its own smoothed version, the signal line. Right now MACD is **3.64** and the signal is **3.82**, so the histogram (the gap between them) is **-0.19**. Two details matter here. MACD is still positive, so the longer trend hasn't turned. And a gap that small says momentum has cooled, not flipped. That fits a stock that ran into the **$345.34** swing high on Sep 22 and has been settling since, with 10-day realised vol running about 25% below the 60-day.

So the honest read is a healthy uptrend that has stopped accelerating. The terminal's own composite lands in the same place: leaning bullish at **+23.5**, conviction low [[src verdict]]. It also flags that the earnings headline from 14 hours ago means the chart is half-weighted, because the trend reading mostly describes the period before it.

What would settle it is price, not argument. A push through **$345.34** that pulls MACD back above its signal line means momentum has caught up with the structure, which is the bullish resolution. A daily close under the 21-day EMA at **$333.75**, with the histogram going further negative, means the MACD was right to flag the fade.
