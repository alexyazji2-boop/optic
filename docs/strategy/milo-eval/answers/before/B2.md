P/E means price divided by earnings. It's what you pay for each $1 the company earned over the past year.

For Apple that's $340.97 divided by $8.72 of earnings per share, which is **39.1** [[src quote]]. Picture buying a coffee shop that earns $1,000 a year. At 39 times earnings you'd pay $39,000, and it would take about 39 years of today's profit to earn that back. (Prices here run about 15 minutes behind.)

Is that high? Yes, by most yardsticks. Apple's own past P/E isn't loaded here. From memory, though, it has spent most of the last ten years below 39, and the broad market has usually traded well below that too. The forward P/E, which uses next year's expected profit of $9.59 instead of last year's, is 35.6. That's a little lower, but still on the high side. Why it matters to you as a holder: a high P/E means the price already counts on good news, so even a decent report can leave the stock flat or lower.

High doesn't automatically mean overpriced. People pay more for faster growth, and Apple's latest figures show earnings up 28.7% and revenue up 16.4% from a year earlier [[src quote]]. The PEG ratio (the P/E divided by the growth rate) is 2.69. Anything much above 1 usually means you're paying a lot for each bit of growth, so the growth is not fully covering the price. What that means for you: the price needs Apple to keep growing at about this pace. The average analyst target is $328.61, below today's price.

The next report is on Nov 2 [[src company]]. That's the moment the market finds out if growth is still keeping up.
