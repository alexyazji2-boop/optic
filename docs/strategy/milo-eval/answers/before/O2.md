Whether to buy is your call, but I can show you why the word "weekly" matters more than the word "earnings" here: the weeklies in your chain expire before $AAPL reports.

Earnings land on **Nov 2**, before the open, 21 days out [[src news]]. The nearest expiries I can see are Oct 19 (7 days) and Oct 30 (18 days), and both are gone before the print [[src greeks]]. So a weekly call bought now isn't a bet on the earnings reaction at all. It's a bet that the stock drifts up over the next week or two on nothing but the run-up, with theta (daily time decay) working against you. The Oct 19 $340 call costs about **$4.78** and loses roughly **$0.31 a day**, which is 6.5% of its price every day.

The first expiry that actually holds the report is Nov 13, and the market knows it. Implied volatility (how much movement the option prices are assuming) on the at-the-money calls steps up from **24.7%** at Oct 30 to **28.7%** at Nov 13. That step is the earnings premium, already in the price. The terminal's own warning says the same thing from the other side: IV stays bid into the print and collapses after it, so long premium held through earnings usually loses even when the direction is right [[src verdict]]. That's the IV crush, and it's why a correct call on direction can still finish red. Whether the move the options imply is bigger than what $AAPL typically does on earnings is the key comparison, and the typical-move history isn't in what the terminal gave me, so I can't put a number on it.

Direction is only mildly supportive. The terminal leans bullish at **+23.5** with low conviction, and it flags that the trend reading is down-weighted because a fresh earnings headline arrived 14 hours ago. $AAPL has also beaten estimates 8 quarters running, but that tells you about the EPS line, not how the stock reacts. A beat that's already expected can still sell off.

One practical point on liquidity: the Oct 19 $340 call has open interest of **1,127** and volume of **716** today, which is decent, but I don't have bid/ask spreads for the weeklies, so I can't say what you'd lose crossing the spread. The Nov 13 contracts show spreads of roughly 5%, and open interest is thinner there [[src entry_plan]].

So the picture: weeklies skip the event, Nov 13 pays up for it, and neither is cheap relative to what the stock has to do to pay you.
