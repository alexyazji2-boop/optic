The S&P is shrugging and the Nasdaq is sulking, and the sulking is mostly chips.

The Nasdaq 100 is down **0.71%** at 30,664.70 while the S&P 500 is down only **0.06%** at 7,807.24, a gap of about 0.65 percentage points [[src macro]]. The overnight futures tell the same story (Nasdaq futures -0.70%, S&P futures -0.15%), so this isn't a quirk of the cash close.

The weight sits in semiconductors. The semiconductor ETF SMH is down **2.05%**, the broader SOXX is down 2.60%, and the memory-chip fund DRAM is down 4.32%, which is a lot of forgotten memories [[src sectors]]. Technology (XLK) is off 1.22% and software (IGV) 1.23%, so the Nasdaq's biggest neighbourhood is red across the board.

The S&P has ballast the Nasdaq doesn't. Energy is up 1.43% and sits at its 52-week high, with health care up 0.69%, utilities up 0.53% and real estate up 0.75%, all groups the Nasdaq 100 holds far less of. That's what turns a tech wobble into a nearly flat S&P and a visibly red Nasdaq. Call it a rotation out of the growth end of the tape, though that's a description of the moves, not a proven cause.

As for why chips are selling, the feed I have here carries no headline for it, so any story I tell is a guess. Rates are standing in the background (the 10-year is at **5.24%**, up 28bp over 20 sessions and near its 52-week high), which is a known headwind for long-duration growth, but the yield slipped slightly today, so it isn't today's trigger [[src macro]].
