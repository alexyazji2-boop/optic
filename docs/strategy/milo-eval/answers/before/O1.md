Close, but not quite: delta is a decent rough guide to the odds of finishing in the money, and that's a different thing from the odds of making money.

Delta (how much the option's price moves per $1 in the stock) happens to double as a rough read on the market-implied chance that the stock finishes above the strike. It's a model number, built from Black-Scholes with a zero interest rate and priced as if everyone were risk-neutral, so treat it as a ballpark, not a forecast. A **0.30** delta says roughly 30% for finishing above **$360**, which is about 5.6% above where $AAPL sits now at **$340.97** [[src quote]].

The catch is that finishing above $360 only gets you to intrinsic value (what the call is worth if exercised, with nothing left in time value). To profit, you have to clear the strike plus whatever you paid. The 360 call I can see in your chain is the Nov 13 one, delta **0.248**, mid **$3.53** [[src strategy_ideas]]. That puts the breakeven near **$363.53**, around 6.6% above spot, so the chance of actually making money at expiry is meaningfully lower than the delta suggests. If your 0.30 is a different expiry, the same logic applies: take the strike, add the premium, and that's the number the stock has to beat.

One more wrinkle, because it's the part people miss. Before expiry you can profit without the stock reaching breakeven, if the option reprices higher on the way. But earnings are on Nov 2, and the terminal flags that implied volatility (the market's priced-in move size) tends to stay bid into the print and then collapse [[src verdict]]. That can eat the gain on a long call even when the stock goes your way.
