A great business at a full price: that's the honest one-liner, and over ten years the price you pay matters far more than anything on a chart.

**What you'd own.** Gross margin is 46.9%, operating margin 32.0% and net margin 26.9% [[src company]]. FY2025 revenue of $416.2B grew 6.4% while net income grew 19.5%, so profit outran sales. The latest quarter was brisker: earnings per share $2.02 against $1.57 a year earlier, up 28.7% [[src quote]]. One blemish: free cash flow slipped from $108.8B to $98.8B in FY2025, down 9.2% on my arithmetic.

**What you'd pay.** $340.97 on $8.72 of trailing earnings is **39.1x** (35.6x forward), a PEG of 2.69, a dividend yield of 0.32%, and an average analyst target of $328.61, below the price [[src quote]]. That's a 2.6% earnings yield against a 10-year Treasury at 5.24% [[src macro]], and a free cash flow yield of roughly 2% on the $4.98T market cap. Apple's own multiple history isn't in what reached me. From memory it sat below 39 for most of the last decade, so by its own standards this is a stretched entry.

**What a decade has to deliver.** This is arithmetic, not a forecast. At a constant price, earnings have to make up for any shrinkage in the multiple. If the multiple settled at 25x, earnings per share would need to reach about $13.64, up 56% from $8.72, just to leave today's price unchanged. That works out to roughly 4.6% a year for ten years. So the question is less whether Apple stays a good business and more whether growth outruns the multiple's drift.

The five-year line shows the path to this price, but it says nothing about the next ten years.

**What I can't see.** Buyback pace, services mix and competitive moat aren't in the data. The headlines are about China, Starlink and iPhone 18 Pro orders, which are noise on a decade horizon. The terminal's "leaning bullish, +23.5" [[src verdict]] is a days-to-weeks reading and doesn't speak to ten years.

**Bottom line.** The read changes if growth slides back toward FY2025's roughly 6% sales pace without the multiple coming down, if gross margin slips below 46.9%, or if free cash flow keeps falling. A multiple that compresses toward earnings, or earnings that grow into it, would soften the objection. How much of your money goes in is yours to decide.
