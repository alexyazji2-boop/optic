Great business, expensive stock. For a ten-year hold the answer turns on the price, because the business half is the easy part.

**The business.** Annual revenue was **$416.2B**, up 6.4%, with net income up 19.5%, a 46.9% gross margin and a 32.0% operating margin [[src company]]. The latest quarter's revenue is up 16.4% on a year earlier [[src quote]], but over the four years Optic has, revenue compounded at just 1.8% a year [[src long_term]]. Free cash flow (cash left after running and reinvesting in the business) was $98.8B, the lowest of those four years. Debt is $98.7B against $35.9B in cash, a $62.7B net debt position [[src company]]. Competitive position and buybacks aren't in the data, so I can't speak to them. The dividend yield is 0.32% [[src quote]].

**What the price assumes.** Trailing P/E is **39.1** against fiscal-year readings of 26.0 to 32.0 and a median of 28.2, which Optic prints as 38.7% above its own history [[src long_term]]. Since the FY2022 average price of $158.63, the stock is up 2.15x while trailing earnings are up 1.43x ($6.11 to $8.72), so much of the gain is investors paying more per dollar of earnings. Forward P/E is 35.6, which assumes about 10% earnings growth ($9.59 against $8.72). The average analyst target is $328.61, under the $340.97 close [[src quote]]. A rising share price describes the stock, not the business.

**What argues against that read.** Four years is a short yardstick, and Optic has no peer multiples, so I can't say whether 39 is high for large tech. Optic's long-term conviction is high (74), but it is mostly trend and relative strength, valuation adds 0 points, and 12 points come from a "resolved catalyst" that is really a recap headline [[src long_term]]. That score summarises past data; it isn't a forecast.

**Bottom line.** The business case is well supported. Whether it's worth this price depends on earnings growing enough over a decade to justify a multiple 38.7% above Apple's own median, and nothing in the data can show that. The read softens if earnings grow into the multiple or the price falls toward its historical range. The decision is yours.
