**Weekly calls into earnings are the most expensive way to make a bet on the print, and the weeklies that expire before Nov 2 don't hold it at all.** This is a read of the data, not a call on what you should do.

Earnings are on Nov 2, 21 days out [[src whats_next]]. The Oct 30 expiry is a Friday that isn't the third one, so it's a weekly, and it ends three days before the report. The first expiry that includes the print is Nov 13.

Here's what the at-the-money 340 call costs at each [[src greeks]]:

- **Oct 30 (18 days):** mid $7.80, delta 0.5304, loses about 2.63% of its value per day to time decay, implied volatility 24.71%. Breakeven is 340 + 7.80 = 347.80, about 2.0% above the 340.97 last close.
- **Nov 13 (32 days):** mid $11.75, implied volatility 28.74%, breakeven 351.75, which is 3.16% above spot [[src naked_ideas]]. The spread is 5.11% of the price, with 232 contracts of open interest.

The jump from about 24.7% to 28.7% implied volatility is the market charging extra for the earnings event. Optic's own warning is that implied volatility stays bid into the print and collapses after it, so long premium held through earnings usually loses even when the direction is right [[src news]]. Options are also priced at **1.50x** realised movement [[src cases]], so you'd be paying up for movement the stock hasn't been delivering. The chart does lean bullish (low conviction, composite +23.5) [[src verdict]], and Apple has beaten estimates 8 quarters in a row [[src company]], but a habitual beater makes a beat the base case rather than news. I don't have bid-ask data for the weeklies, only for Nov 13.

**What would change the read:** a cheaper implied volatility relative to realised movement, or a close above the 350 call wall, where dealer hedging currently leans against rallies [[src gex]].
