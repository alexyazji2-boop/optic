**The business.** Margins are the headline: gross 46.9%, operating 32.0% and net 26.9% [[src company]]. Annual net income grew 19.5% on revenue growth of 6.4%, so profits are growing faster than sales. The balance sheet carries $98.66B of debt against $35.93B of cash, a net debt of $62.72B. Optic's long-term conviction score is high at 74, driven mostly by the long trend and five-year outperformance against SPY [[src long_term]]. Buybacks, dividends beyond the 0.32% yield and competitive position aren't in the data on this page, so I can't speak to them.

**The price.** This is where the answer turns. Trailing P/E is **39.1 against a median of 28.2** across its last four reported years, **38.7% above** its own norm, and the forward P/E is 35.6 [[src long_term]]. The analyst target of $328.61 sits below the 340.97 close [[src quote]]. A great business and a good price are separate questions: the first looks like a yes, and on the second the stock is asking for a premium, though modesty is hard to find at a market value of about $4.98 trillion. A fair caveat is that four years is a short history for a "norm".

**What could change the read:** a P/E drifting back toward its median, either through price or through earnings catching up, or a break in the margins above. The next report is Nov 2 [[src next_earnings_date]]. That's the data, not advice on what to hold.
