P/E (price-to-earnings ratio) is what you pay for each dollar the company earns in a year, and yes, Apple's is high against its own past.

Think of buying a small shop. If it earns $1 a year and costs $39 to buy, that shop has a P/E of 39. Apple's is **39.1** [[src quote]], found by dividing the $340.97 price by earnings of $8.72 per share.

Over the last four fiscal years Apple's P/E ranged from 26.0 to 32.0, with a median of 28.2. Today's is **38.7%** above that median [[src long_term]]. Why it matters if you hold it: a high P/E means the price already counts on strong growth, so there's less room for a disappointment. Only four years of history are available, and a P/E tells you nothing about how Apple compares with other companies.
