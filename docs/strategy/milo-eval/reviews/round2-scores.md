## Errors

**B1, Answer A**
- "Apple's own multiple history isn't in what I have loaded, but from memory it spent most of the last decade well below 39." The admission is honest, but the recalled history is presented as support for the valuation argument. That is an evidence problem under the brief. (The own-history median is presumably what didn't reach this version.)
- "Profit margin is 27.6%". The data gives a net margin of about 27% (26.9%), so 27.6% doesn't match. It may be a different margin definition, but it isn't labeled as one.

**B2, Answer B**
- "From memory, though, it has spent most of the last ten years below 39, and the broad market has usually traded well below that too." This is recalled history and market comparison used in place of data. The own-history median (28.2) is what the check calls for.
- "it would take about 39 years of today's profit to earn that back". This payback framing assumes profits never grow, so for a beginner it overstates how expensive the stock is. The answer does partly correct this later.
- "Anything much above 1 usually means you're paying a lot for each bit of growth". The PEG>1 rule of thumb is stated loosely and adds jargon the "simple" setting didn't need. It's a minor point.

**D1, Answer A**
- "CPI and an options expiry land on Wednesday Oct 14". The October monthly options expire Fri Oct 16. If the data held a Wednesday weekly expiry, the answer should say so; as written, it reads as the main October expiry on the wrong date.

**D1, Answer B**
- "ATR ... is $5.59, so a normal day can cover that whole stack and still not clear it." This contradicts itself: if a normal day's range ($5.59) is bigger than the $4.44 stack, an average move could clear it. The conclusion doesn't follow from the numbers.
- "spot is 9% above the $206.06 flip". Spot is 9.8% above the flip; the flip is 9.0% below spot. The direction of the percentage is swapped, which is a minor slip.

**D2, Answer B**
- "spot 8.95% above the $206.06 flip". 226.32/206.06 = +9.8%. The 8.95% figure is the flip's distance below spot, measured from spot. It's a minor computational slip.

**S1, Answer A**
- "**Levels CONTEXT computed.**" This internal prompt label leaked into a user-facing heading. It's a clarity defect, not a factual error.

**S2, Answer A**
- "Part of its lift comes from one headline tagged 'earnings' that looks like a recap". In the short-term composite (+23.5), the earnings tag halved the technicals weight, which lowered the score (about +34.7 at nominal weights). The headline cut the lift, it didn't add to it. The 12-point catalyst boost belongs to the separate long-term conviction score, so the two scores are conflated.

**L1, Answer A**
- "Apple's own multiple history isn't in what reached me. From memory it sat below 39 for most of the last decade". The admission is honest, but recalled history is then used as the basis for calling the entry "stretched by its own standards". That is an evidence problem. (The own-history median of 28.2 is what the check asks for.)
- "if gross margin slips below 46.9%". That is the current value, so the threshold is arbitrary: any decline at all would trip it. It's a minor point.

**L1, Answer B**
- "$98.7B of debt against $35.9B of cash, a net debt of $62.72B". 98.7 - 35.9 = 62.8. The figure is trivially inconsistent with the rounded inputs. It's minor.

**L2, Answer B**
- "One correction first. The score in front of me isn't high." The user was asking about the long-term conviction score (74, "high"), which is real. Calling this a "correction" implies the user was wrong. Saying the figure didn't reach this version is honest; framing that as a correction is not.

**X1, Answer A**
- "the base rate favors continuation until it breaks". This is a generic momentum claim with no supporting data, and it reads as a forecast. Optic's signals are not validated as predicting returns.
- "1.3% below the $345.34 swing high, which is also where a three-touch resistance band sits". The three-touch resistance is at $344.18, not $345.34. It's a minor conflation.

**X1, Answer B**
- "part of the score's lift comes from a headline tagged 'earnings'" (cited to the short-term verdict). In the short-term composite, the tag halved the technicals weight and lowered the score (about +34.7 at nominal weights vs +23.5). This is the same conflation with the long-term score's 12 catalyst points as S2-A.
- "a **27.6%** profit margin". Net margin in the data is about 27% (26.9%). The answer doesn't say which margin it means.

**X2, Answer A**
- "dealer hedging should dampen moves from here, with the call wall at $245 capping the top". GEX is stated without the model-assumption caveat, and "capping" overstates what the call wall can do.
- "this is a stock settling after the run ..., not one being sold". Falling realised vol is consistent with this, but it doesn't establish it. Interpretation is presented as fact.

**T1, Answer A**
- "the data on this page was fetched about 238 minutes ago". The header the assistant saw said 231 minutes. The figure is wrong, though the overall staleness warning is right.

**T1, Answer B**
- "leaning bullish, **+23.5 out of 100**". The composite is on a -100 to +100 scale; on a 0-100 scale the same reading is 62. "Out of 100" leads a reader to think it's 23.5/100, which reads as bearish.

**F2, Answer A**
- "200-day (**$202.34**, about 12% below spot)". The 200-day is 10.6% below spot; spot is 11.9% above it. The base of the percentage is swapped. It's minor.
