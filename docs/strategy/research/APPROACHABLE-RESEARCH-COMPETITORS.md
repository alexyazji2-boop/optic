## 1. Simply Wall St (SWS)

Sources (all read 2026-10-12): plans page https://simplywall.st/plans ; Company Analysis Model docs https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SimplyWallSt/Company-Analysis-Model/master/MODEL.markdown ; Snowflake help article https://support.simplywall.st/hc/en-us/articles/360001740916 (403 on direct fetch; content via search snippet); Narratives https://www.simplywall.st/features/community-narratives ; Watchlist https://simplywall.st/features/stock-watchlist ; Charlie https://simplywall.st/features/ask-charlie ; reviews https://walletinvestor.com/news/ai-news/simply-wall-st-review-how-accurate-are-the-ai-driven-snowflake-visual-analysis-models/ , https://completetradersedge.com/simply-wall-st-review/ , https://stockunlock.com/simply-wall-st-review.html , Trustpilot https://www.trustpilot.com/review/simplywall.st . Note: public stock report pages returned HTTP 403 to my fetcher, so no OBS of the report layout; layout below is from docs, marketing and reviews.

1. **Audience / promise.** Long-term, fundamentals-oriented retail investors who are not spreadsheet users; the model docs say the aim is long-term, non-emotional decisions grounded in fundamentals [DOC, MODEL.markdown]. Reviewers consistently frame it as "visual fundamentals for beginners" and say Excel/DCF users outgrow it within a year [3P, completetradersedge].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Ticker -> Snowflake (a five-axis shape: Value, Future Growth, Past Performance, Financial Health, Dividends) -> Rewards & Risks list -> drill into each axis section where every check is a plain pass/fail statement -> set a fair value (analyst target, SWS DCF, or a community Narrative) on a watchlist -> get notified when price crosses it or the Narrative updates [DOC, MODEL.markdown; MKT, stock-watchlist].
3. **Time to first insight.** Seconds: the shape and colour (bigger and greener = more checks passed) is the first thing a user reads [DOC, MODEL.markdown]. Free accounts get 5 company reports/month, so first insight is fast but rationed [DOC, plans].
4. **Hierarchy / jargon.** Each axis has exactly 6 binary checks, scored 1/0, max 6 per axis [DOC, MODEL.markdown + help article]. Checks are phrased as questions a beginner can follow (e.g. "is the stock trading well below DCF fair value?", "do short-term assets exceed short-term liabilities?", "is interest covered 5x by earnings?") [DOC, MODEL.markdown, paraphrased]. **Quality vs valuation separation is structural:** Value is the only axis built on a valuation model (DCF plus P/E, PEG, P/B vs peers/market); Growth uses analyst forecasts, Past and Health use historicals [DOC]. The docs explicitly warn a low axis is not disqualifying (low Dividends may just mean a reinvesting growth company) [DOC]. Management (CEO pay, tenure, insider trading) is "flagged" not scored, signalling concern rather than points [DOC]. Risks are labelled Major or Minor with a dated "New Risk" note and one-line reason (e.g. under 1 year of cash runway; more volatile than 90% of UK stocks) [OBS of search-indexed report snippets, simplywall.st/ASX:EWC etc.; the severity rules themselves are not publicly documented, and the same check appears as major for one company and minor for another, INF].
5. **Workflows.** Screener (free: top 4 results only), portfolio with transaction-based "true return" (praised by reviewers), watchlists (3 x 50), fair-value alerts [DOC plans; 3P completetradersedge]. **Narratives:** community-written theses, each with summary, key points, revenue growth assumption and a resulting Narrative Fair Value vs price; users follow narratives, can create their own "in minutes", and can adopt a narrative's fair value as their alert target [MKT, community-narratives; stock-watchlist]. This is the closest thing in the category to structured thesis tracking. Disclaimers note authors' scenarios are not recommendations; staff authors are labelled [MKT].
6. **AI (Charlie).** Marketed as a background "investing agent": reviews the portfolio every morning, tracks news and moves affecting holdings, considers your portfolio, saved companies and preferences, and can watch a screen you are viewing and surface new ideas [MKT, ask-charlie]. Claims every figure links to its source and you can open the calculation behind it; examples state what would change its view and quantify sector weight with a trade-off [MKT]. Users choose what Charlie can access and change [MKT]. Plan inclusion not stated on the page [MKT]. No independent quality testing found [INF].
7. **Mobile.** Native iOS/Android apps exist; reviews treat mobile as a first-class surface [3P, stockunlock]. No continuity details documented [INF].
8. **Recurring value / packaging.** Free / Premium / Unlimited; 7-day Premium trial then auto-drop to Free [DOC, plans]. Free: 5 reports/mo, 1 portfolio x 10 holdings, 3 watchlists x 50, top-4 screener results, 12-month equity curve, "limited" portfolio/watchlist updates; Premium: 30 reports, 3 portfolios x 30, "Smart" updates; Unlimited: unlimited [DOC, plans]. Price not rendered to my fetcher; third parties report about $131/yr Premium and $258/yr Unlimited, annual billing pushed, 14-day refund [3P, completetradersedge / toolchase]. Upgrade triggers: report quota, 10-holding cap, 4-result screener, "Smart" updates [INF from DOC]. Return reasons: fair-value-crossed alerts, Narrative updates, daily Charlie review [MKT].
9. **Weaknesses.** Snowflake is a scorecard, not a forecast; no published hit rate showing high-scoring snowflakes outperform [3P, walletinvestor]. Auto-generated DCF can be optimistic and is hard to edit [3P, completetradersedge/stockunlock]. Billing/auto-renewal and data-gap complaints [3P, Trustpilot summary]. No technical/options/swing workflow at all [INF]. Binary checks hide magnitude (a near-miss and a disaster both score 0) [INF].

**Journeys.** J1 unfamiliar company: best-in-class first glance (shape + risks), but quota-limited [DOC/INF]. J2 swing thesis: not served; long-term fundamentals only, minor price moves deliberately ignored [DOC]. J3 sudden move: Charlie claims news tracking for holdings; reports refresh risks daily, but no "why is it moving" view documented [MKT/INF]. J4 exposure: portfolio with sector/true-return views; Charlie quantifies sector concentration [3P/MKT].

## 2. Seeking Alpha (SA)

Sources (read 2026-10-12): symbol-page help https://help.seekingalpha.com/premium/how-to-assess-any-stock-using-premium-features-on-symbol-summary-pages ; Quant FAQ https://help.seekingalpha.com/premium/quant-ratings-and-factor-grades-faq (via search snippet) ; Virtual Analyst/Summary Report https://help.seekingalpha.com/what-is-the-virtual-analyst-report and https://help.seekingalpha.com/migration/does-seeking-alpha-use-ai-to-generate-these-reports ; Ask SA https://help.seekingalpha.com/what-is-ask-seeking-alpha-or-ask-sa ; Premium feature list https://help.seekingalpha.com/premium/seeking-alpha-premium-feature-list ; alerts https://help.seekingalpha.com/basic/how-do-i-manage-my-email-alerts ; offer page https://subscriptions.seekingalpha.com/lp_premium_0/ ; reviews https://completetradersedge.com/seeking-alpha-review/ , https://daytradingz.com/seeking-alpha-review/ , https://financer.com/invest/seeking-alpha-premium-review/ . Symbol pages returned 403 to my fetcher: no OBS.

1. **Audience / promise.** Self-directed long-term and swing investors who want crowd-sourced analysis plus a mechanical rating; Premium pitches "data-driven stock ratings" and 4,000+ covered names incl. 1,300 Wall Street ignores [MKT, lp_premium_0]. Reviewers say it is built for investors, not day traders [3P, daytradingtoolkit via search].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Symbol page -> Ratings Summary (three ratings side by side: Quant, SA Analysts, Wall Street sell-side) -> factor grades -> Bulls Say / Bears Say -> filter articles by author rating -> add to portfolio -> rating-change alerts and portfolio health score [DOC, symbol-page help; feature list].
3. **Time to first insight.** Very fast for subscribers (a 1.0-5.0 score and letter grades on the first screen) [DOC]. For free users the first meaningful insight is behind a paywall: reviews say the free tier hits the wall as soon as you click a Premium article or Quant rating [3P, completetradersedge]; app listing says free = 1 Premium article/month [3P via app listing snippet].
4. **Hierarchy / jargon.** Five factor grades (Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum, EPS Revisions), each A+ to F **relative to sector peers**, built from 100+ metrics; overall Quant rating is not an average but weights the most predictive metrics [DOC, Quant FAQ]. SA explicitly explains the role split: Value/Growth/Profitability find mispricing, Momentum/Revisions signal timing [DOC]. That is a clean "what is it worth / is it a good business / is now the time" decomposition, though "Value" here is relative multiples, not intrinsic value [INF]. Clicking a blue grade opens the metric drill-down (progressive disclosure) [DOC]. Separate Dividend Safety/Growth grades [DOC, about.seekingalpha.com/quant-dividend-grades]. Quote for the tone: quant ratings are framed as "an objective, unemotional evaluation" [DOC, symbol-page help]. Little evidence of in-context glossary or plain-English rewrites; grades presume the user knows what EPS revisions are [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Screener on ratings/factor grades; Top-rated lists; compare 6 stocks with grades, export to spreadsheet/PDF; 10 years of statements; author rating history; broker-linked portfolio with daily sync; price and rating alerts; portfolio health score = aggregate Quant of holdings [DOC, feature list]. Email center splits Content, Ratings & Price, Analyst, and **Portfolio Digests** (a summary mode for people who want fewer emails) [DOC, email alerts help].
6. **AI.** (a) **Virtual Analyst / Summary Report**: LLM-written five-section overview (Company Description; Competitors and Unique Value; Rating explanation; Positives Raised by Analysts; Concerns Raised by Analysts), generated from SA site content, not reviewed by editors, may contain errors, regenerated on a schedule so check the creation date [DOC, two help pages]. Strong pattern: the bull/bear sections are attributed to named analysts' articles, so evidence = the crowd's arguments, not the model's opinion [DOC/INF]. (b) **Earnings Call Insights** [MKT]. (c) **Ask SA**, an AI search assistant over SA data, ratings and research [DOC]; tier is inconsistent across sources (Premium page lists it; a Nov 2025 SA video says expanded data integration is for PRO; a third-party review says PRO-only) [MKT/3P]. No documented page or holdings awareness [DOC gap].
7. **Mobile.** Mature native apps; the Quant FAQ (2024) said ratings were desktop-first with mobile planned later [DOC]. Alerts reach phones via push [DOC].
8. **Packaging.** Basic (free), Premium $299/yr (1-month $4.95 paid trial, auto-renews annually, no monthly plan after trial), Alpha Picks $499/yr (2 quant-picked stocks/month), PRO ~$2,400/yr [MKT, lp_premium_0; 3P, financer]. Upgrade triggers: clicking a Premium article, a Quant rating, factor grade, or screener filter [3P]. Return reasons: rating-change alerts, portfolio digests, new articles on holdings, Wall Street Breakfast newsletter [DOC/INF].
9. **Weaknesses.** Paywall fatigue; contributor quality varies widely, so readers must judge authors [3P, completetradersedge]. Quant method only partly disclosed (weights unknown); performance claims rest largely on SA-promoted studies [3P]. Annual auto-renew complaints [3P]. Grades describe, but do not teach; beginners get letters without the "so what" [INF].

**Journeys.** J1: three-way rating + Bulls/Bears + AI summary is strong but paywalled [DOC]. J2 swing thesis: Momentum and Revisions grades are explicitly timing factors; articles give theses; no thesis object or journal [DOC/INF]. J3 sudden move: news feed and articles; SA "news" items explain moves quickly (well known; not verified here) [INF]. J4 exposure: portfolio health score and broker sync, but health = average rating, not concentration [DOC/INF].

## 3. Morningstar (Morningstar Investor)

Sources (read 2026-10-12/13): Equity Research Methodology (2023-06-14) https://s205.q4cdn.com/437373358/files/doc_downloads/2025/07/Morningstar-Equity-Research-Methodology-2023-06-14.pdf (via search summaries) ; regional explainer https://www.morningstar.in/posts/28684/6-key-concepts-to-stock-investing-defined.aspx ; "Putting fair value estimates in context" https://morningstar.ca/ca/news/185410/putting-fair-value-estimates-in-context.aspx ; Bulls/Bears glossary https://awgmain.morningstar.com/webhelp/glossary_definitions/indexes/stkmorningstaranalysis.html ; Investor portfolio page https://www.morningstar.com/mm/investor/portfolio-management-tools ; Mo press release https://newsroom.morningstar.com/newsroom/news-archive/press-release-details/2023/Mo-an-AI-Chatbot-Powered-by-Morningstar-Intelligence-Engine-Debuts-in-Morningstar-Platforms/default.aspx ; advisor AI assistant (Mar 2026) https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260309307944/en ; reviews https://robberger.com/morningstar-investor-review-and-users-guide/ , https://tickernerd.com/resources/morningstar-review/ . Stock quote pages returned 403 to my fetcher: no OBS.

1. **Audience / promise.** Long-term investors (funds, ETFs, stocks) who want independent analyst opinion; the product's center of gravity is "what is this business worth, and how durable is it" [DOC, methodology]. Investopedia praise is reused in marketing [MKT, portfolio page].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Stock page -> star rating (1-5) -> Fair Value vs price -> Economic Moat (None/Narrow/Wide) + Uncertainty (Low ... Extreme) + Capital Allocation -> Bulls Say / Bears Say -> full analyst note -> portfolio X-Ray to see what you really own [DOC, methodology + glossary; MKT, portfolio page].
3. **Time to first insight.** One glance for subscribers (stars + price/fair value). Free access is thin: reviewers say only editorial articles are fully free and new users may not get a free Basic account at all [3P, robberger]. 7-day trial [MKT].
4. **Hierarchy / jargon. The clearest quality-vs-valuation separation in the category.** Business quality = Moat (durable advantage, defined as ROIC above WACC; narrow = more likely than not to earn excess returns for 10 years, wide = very high confidence of 10 years) and Capital Allocation (management's stewardship). Valuation = Fair Value from analyst DCF. Confidence = Uncertainty rating, which sets the required margin of safety. Stars combine them: the bigger the risk-adjusted discount, the more stars; 3 stars = fairly valued; a high-uncertainty stock needs a bigger discount to earn 4-5 stars [DOC, methodology; morningstar.in explainer]. So a wide-moat company can be 1-star (great business, too expensive), and this is explained in consumer articles [DOC/INF]. Moat sources are named in plain language (switching costs, cost advantage, intangible assets/brands, network effects, efficient scale) [DOC]. Bulls Say / Bears Say = the most important arguments for and against owning [DOC, glossary]. Morningstar also stresses fair value is an estimate of intrinsic business value, not what others will pay short term [DOC, morningstar.ca]. Ratings update each evening after the close as price moves vs fair value [DOC, methodology]. Quote: moat is a feature letting a firm "sustain excess profits over a long period of time" [DOC, methodology].
5. **Workflows.** Screener (200+ filters per reviews), watchlists, compare, notifications, Portfolio with account linking or manual entry, **X-Ray** (allocation, sector weights, fees, style) and **Stock Intersection** (overlap across funds/stocks, i.e. true concentration) [MKT, portfolio page; 3P, robberger]. Limits reported: 25 portfolios, X-Ray max 150 holdings [3P, forum quoting help center, may be stale].
6. **AI.** "Mo" chatbot launched in beta 2023 across Investor and advisor platforms, grounded in Morningstar research, explicitly no advice [DOC, press release]. A 2025 review calls it limited [3P, tickernerd]. In March 2026 Morningstar launched a new AI assistant in its advisor suite (flags meaningful rating changes, prepares briefs, portfolio analysis) replacing Mo there; no evidence yet for the retail Investor app [DOC, businesswire]. Retail AI is therefore a gap [INF].
7. **Mobile.** Apps exist; reviewers treat desktop as primary for X-Ray [3P/INF].
8. **Packaging.** Investor $249/yr ($20.75/mo) or $34.95/mo; 7-day trial, one per user [MKT, portfolio page]. Upgrade triggers: any rating, fair value, analyst note, X-Ray [3P]. Return reasons: rating changes, portfolio monitoring, editorial [INF].
9. **Weaknesses.** Long-horizon fair values can lag fast-moving markets; historic criticism of bullish financials ratings pre-2008 and an older WSJ-reported pressure allegation that management denied [3P, integrity-research summary]. Expensive with near-zero free tier [3P]. Analyst coverage is limited to ~1,500-1,700 stocks (others get quantitative ratings) [INF from general knowledge, not verified this session]. No technicals or trading workflow; AI lags [INF].

**Journeys.** J1: excellent if covered and paid: moat + fair value + bulls/bears answers "good business? good price?" [DOC]. J2 swing: not served [INF]. J3 sudden move: analyst notes after earnings, otherwise news; no "why is it moving" feature found [INF]. J4 exposure: best-in-class (X-Ray + Stock Intersection reveal hidden overlap via funds) [MKT].

## 4. Yahoo Finance

Sources (read 2026-10-12/13): **OBS** of https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AAPL/ (logged-out fetch, page text) and https://finance.yahoo.com/plans/ ; Yahoo Scout press release https://unified.www.yahooinc.com/press/introducing-yahoo-scout-a-new-ai-answer-engine (Jan 27 2026, via search summary) ; iOS notifications help https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN28269.html ; 2023 redesign release https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231107696257/en ; pricing aggregator https://recurdash.com/subscription-pricing/yahoo-finance .

1. **Audience / promise.** The default free quote page for retail; everyone starts here, and Optic's own data comes from Yahoo via yfinance [INF; brief]. 2023 redesign was pitched at "everyday investors" [MKT, businesswire].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Search ticker -> quote + chart -> **AI summary "What's happening with AAPL?"** -> key stats -> news -> analyst targets -> portfolio/watchlist with push alerts [OBS, AAPL page].
3. **Time to first insight.** Seconds and free, no account: the Scout AI summary sits directly under the chart, above key stats, with suggested follow-ups such as why the stock is up today [OBS]. This is now the free baseline for J3.
4. **Hierarchy / jargon.** Order observed logged-out: market strip -> quote header (incl. overnight Blue Ocean session note) -> broker promo -> chart (1D-All, links to Advanced/AlphaSpace charts) -> **Scout AI summary** -> key stats (beta, P/E, EPS, 1y target etc.) -> company profile -> videos -> news tabs (News, Earnings Calls, Press Releases, SEC Filings) -> performance vs S&P 500 -> earnings trends -> analyst insights (price target range, latest rating actions) -> valuation statistics (with gated quarterly toggle) -> compare up to 4 peers -> **Company insights: Fair Value, Dividend Score, Hiring Score, Insider Sentiment** (gated) -> Argus and Morningstar research summaries -> "People also watch" [OBS]. Jargon handling: short tooltips on the proprietary scores (e.g. Fair Value = appropriate price based on earnings and growth with a 5-year estimated return) but standard stats (beta, EPS) are bare labels [OBS]. Quality vs valuation is not separated; it is a flat data page [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Watchlists, portfolios (sign-in), push alerts for news and significant price moves on followed symbols [DOC, SLN28269], price/earnings alerts per App Store listing [MKT]. Screener; Gold adds AlphaSpace platform, 50+ chart patterns, Smart Money screener, CSV downloads, ~40 years statements [MKT, plans].
6. **AI (Yahoo Scout).** Launched Jan 27 2026 as a free US answer engine; built on Anthropic's Claude as primary model with Bing grounding for sources; in Finance it embeds in articles and quote pages, refreshes about every 10 minutes with headlines and **stock-movement explanations**, and offers AI follow-up questions [DOC/MKT, Yahoo press release; OBS of quote page]. No evidence it sees the user's portfolio [INF]. Free and ubiquitous [DOC].
7. **Mobile.** Mature apps, push notifications, multiple watchlists [DOC/MKT]. Continuity via Yahoo account [INF].
8. **Packaging.** Plans page: Bronze $95.40/yr ($7.95/mo billed yearly), Silver $239.40/yr ($19.95/mo, "Most Popular"), Gold $479.40/yr ($39.95/mo, 7-day trial) [OBS, plans]; monthly billing $9.95/$24.95/$49.95 [3P, recurdash]. Bronze = ad-free + portfolio risk/diversification views + community sentiment; Silver = Morningstar ratings, Argus/Morningstar reports, fair value, dividend scores, expert picks, model portfolios; Gold = AlphaSpace, patterns, Smart Money screener, CSV [OBS, plans]. **Notable: Yahoo resells Morningstar ratings** in Silver [OBS]. Paywall moments seen in-page: gated Fair Value/Insights tiles, research reports, valuation toggle [OBS].
9. **Weaknesses.** Dense, ad-heavy, data dump with no narrative or teaching; page even rendered an error banner on fetch [OBS]. Broker referral promo above chart [OBS]. Scout explains today but does not track a thesis or tie to holdings [INF].

**Journeys.** J1: profile + stats + AI summary; no "is this a good business" framing [OBS/INF]. J2 swing: charts and patterns (Gold); no thesis object [MKT/INF]. J3: **now strongest free answer** via Scout movement explanations [DOC/OBS]. J4: Bronze diversification/volatility views [OBS, plans].

## 5. Robinhood Cortex (beginner AI layer) + AI thesis trackers (thesis-tracking reference)

Chosen because Robinhood is where the largest pool of beginner investors already is, and Cortex is the mass-market version of what Milo is trying to be; and because the "investment thesis tracking" job is now being productized by small AI tools, which no incumbent above does well.

### 5a. Robinhood Cortex
Sources (read 2026-10-13): Cortex help https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/cortex ; Digests newsroom https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/digests-by-robinhood-cortex-uk (Aug 19 2025) ; Gold page https://robinhood.com/gold ; Benzinga https://benzinga.com/markets/tech/25/08/47252402/ ; Finder Gold test https://www.finder.com/investments/is-robinhood-gold-worth-it-experiment .

1. **Audience / promise.** Robinhood's retail base; Cortex is an "AI investing assistant" whose job is explaining why things move and what it means for your holdings [DOC, help; MKT].
2. **Workflow.** Stock detail page -> **Digests**: plain-English summary of why a stock may be moving, built from breaking news, analyst reports, technical indicators and Robinhood data; covers many commonly traded stocks, not all [DOC, newsroom]. Then Cortex assistant: plain-language Q&A with follow-ups and voice; builds screeners showing criteria, list and data source; drafts market/stop/limit orders for user review (cannot submit; links out for complex/multi-leg) [DOC, help].
3. **Time to insight.** Zero setup: Digests sits on the page users already open before trading [DOC]. Company reports 95% of surveyed US users like it [MKT, self-reported].
4. **Jargon.** Plain English is the explicit design goal [DOC]. No quality-vs-valuation framework [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Monitoring of market moves and breaking news affecting the portfolio; screeners; order drafting [DOC].
6. **AI.** **Portfolio-aware** (uses portfolio and account context) [DOC]. Explicit refusals: no buy/sell recommendations or price predictions; does not browse the open web or use social sentiment [DOC]. Must accept a Cortex agreement on first use; chats saved 30 days [DOC]. Order drafting with human confirm is the same pattern as Milo's confirm-before-action [DOC/INF].
7. **Mobile.** iOS app first (disclosure says available via iOS app) [DOC].
8. **Packaging.** Cortex assistant requires **Gold, $5/mo or $50/yr**, 30-day free trial; Digests launched free (UK) and rolled out in US in 2025 [DOC, help; newsroom]. Gold bundles cash yield, margin, research, so AI is a sweetener, not the product [MKT/INF].
9. **Weaknesses.** Tied to the broker; explains moves but does not teach business quality or valuation, and no thesis memory [INF]. Encourages acting (order drafts) at the moment of the move [INF].

Journeys: J3 is its core [DOC]; J4 via portfolio-aware chat [DOC]; J1/J2 thin [INF].

### 5b. AI thesis trackers (AllMind Thesis Monitor, FN2 Thesis Tracker) and trade journals
Sources: AllMind docs https://complete-docs-x17s80.allmind.ai/docs/thesis-monitoring/guide ; FN2 https://fn2.ai/agents/thesis-tracker ; Edgewonk pricing https://edgewonk.com/pricing ; Tradervue https://tradervue.com/site/edgewonk-alternative (all read 2026-10-13).

- **AllMind Thesis Monitor** [DOC]: user writes the thesis **and "what would prove it wrong"** (the disconfirming signals are watched most closely); picks type (trade/company/event/theme), tickers and position; re-check cadence 6h to 7d (default daily); alert sensitivity Early / Standard / Strict (weakening / challenged / broken), firing only when a verdict first crosses the threshold. Each review outputs a verdict (Holding, Weakening, Challenged, Broken, Untested, Inconclusive) plus an evidence-quality read (strong/mixed/thin), "what changed" items tagged supporting/pressuring/neutral, **pillars** each marked confirmed/stable/weakening/at-risk/refuted, confirming vs disconfirming evidence side by side, upcoming catalysts, exportable memo. Sources: filings, transcripts, press releases, estimates, prices, news, web search. Note it also suggests actions (hold/reduce/hedge/exit), which Optic should not copy for retail.
- **FN2 Thesis Tracker** [MKT]: thesis in plain English; supporting and contradicting evidence, each sourced, contradicting side surfaced on purpose; items tagged bullish/bearish/watch rolling into a thesis-health read; weekly "what changed" showing only new evidence; scheduled runs with email; "free to start"; educational, not advice.
- **Trade journals (Edgewonk, Tradervue)** [3P/DOC]: per-trade notes, custom tags, setups and mistake tracking, screenshots, setup checklists, performance by tag; Edgewonk ~$197/yr. They journal executed trades, not the research thesis before the trade [INF].
- **Implication** [INF]: the thesis-with-falsifier + scheduled evidence check + "what changed" pattern is new, AI-native, and unclaimed by any mainstream approachable product (SWS Narratives come closest but are community fair-value models, not personal falsifiable theses).

## Cross-cutting answers to the brief's focus questions

- **Jargon in context.** Best: SWS turns every metric into a pass/fail sentence a beginner can read [DOC]; Morningstar names moat sources in everyday words [DOC]; Robinhood Digests are plain-English by design [DOC]. Yahoo gives tooltips only on its own proprietary scores [OBS]. SA grades are letters with metric drill-downs but little teaching [DOC/INF]. Nobody documented an "explain this term at my level" control; Milo's "Explain this" is ahead here if it stays short [INF].
- **Quality vs valuation.** Morningstar is the reference model: Moat + Capital Allocation (quality) vs Fair Value (price) vs Uncertainty (confidence), combined into stars only at the end [DOC]. SWS separates Value from Health/Past/Growth axes [DOC]. SA separates mispricing factors from timing factors [DOC]. Yahoo and Robinhood do not separate [OBS/INF].
- **Bull/bear with evidence.** Morningstar Bulls Say/Bears Say (analyst-written) [DOC]; SA Bulls Say/Bears Say and AI "Positives/Concerns Raised by Analysts", attributed to the crowd's articles [DOC]; SWS dated Major/Minor risks with a one-line numeric reason [OBS-snippets]; AllMind/FN2 confirming vs disconfirming evidence, each sourced [DOC/MKT].
- **What a beginner sees first.** SWS: a shape. Morningstar: stars. SA: a 1-5 score. Yahoo: price, chart, then an AI "what's happening" box. Robinhood: a Digest of why it is moving. Every product leads with one compressed verdict or explanation, not a data grid [INF].
- **Reason to return.** Rating-change alerts (SA, Morningstar), fair-value-crossed and Narrative-updated alerts (SWS), daily AI portfolio review (SWS Charlie), portfolio digests email (SA), news/price push (Yahoo, Robinhood) [DOC/MKT]. Thesis-health "what changed this week" (FN2, AllMind) [DOC/MKT].
- **Thesis tracking / notes.** Only SWS Narratives (community fair-value theses you can follow and alert on) among mainstream tools; AI-native trackers are niche [MKT/DOC].
- **Packaging and paywall moments.** Price ladder: Robinhood Gold $50/yr; Yahoo Bronze $95/yr; SWS Premium ~$131/yr; Yahoo Silver $239/yr; Morningstar $249/yr; SA Premium $299/yr; SWS Unlimited ~$258/yr; Yahoo Gold $479/yr; SA Alpha Picks $499/yr; SA PRO ~$2,400/yr [OBS/MKT/3P]. Paywall is triggered at the verdict (rating, fair value, grade), at quotas (SWS 5 reports/mo, 10 holdings, top-4 screen results) or at the second article (SA) [DOC/3P]. Annual auto-renew is the most common complaint (SWS, SA) [3P].
- **Criticisms.** Scorecards without published hit rates (SWS) [3P]; partly opaque weighting (SA Quant) [3P]; auto DCF optimistic/uneditable (SWS) [3P]; variable contributor quality (SA) [3P]; long-horizon fair values lag (Morningstar) [3P]; AI reports unreviewed and possibly days old (SA, self-disclosed) [DOC].

## Baseline expectations (what users will assume exists)

1. One compressed verdict at the top of a company page (shape, stars, score) with a drill-down to its parts [DOC, all].
2. A bull and a bear case, each with evidence, on every covered name [DOC, SA/Morningstar].
3. A free plain-English "why is it moving" explanation (Yahoo Scout and Robinhood Digests made this free) [OBS/DOC].
4. Watchlist + price alerts + push notifications; rating/score-change alerts on paid tiers [DOC, SA/Yahoo/SWS].
5. Portfolio view with sector allocation and diversification/concentration [MKT, Yahoo Bronze, Morningstar, SWS].
6. AI output with dates and sources, and an explicit "not advice" boundary [DOC, SA/Robinhood/SWS].
7. A mobile app or a phone-first experience with notifications [DOC/MKT].
8. A free tier that shows enough to judge, with the paywall at depth, quota, or verdict; 7-30 day trials [DOC].

## Proven ideas worth adapting (for Optic's plain-English audience)

1. **Checks as sentences** (SWS): render each component of the Optic Score as a short pass/fail or better/worse sentence with the number behind it, e.g. "Cash covers 2 years of losses" instead of "Current ratio 1.8". Keep magnitude visible (SWS's binary hides near-misses).
2. **Two cards, never one blend** (Morningstar): "Is it a good business?" (durability, margins, balance sheet) and "Is the price reasonable?" (vs its own history and peers), plus a "How sure can anyone be?" label from volatility/estimate dispersion. State openly that the two can disagree. Avoid a fake intrinsic value Optic cannot defend; use ranges.
3. **Business vs timing language** (SA): relabel the short-term / long-term score split as "the business" vs "the setup right now", so swing traders and long-term holders each know which to read.
4. **Provenance and freshness on every AI paragraph** (SA's own disclosures): "written 2h ago from 10-Q, 3 news items"; link each claim to the figure ("Explain this" already does part of this).
5. **Dated, new-since-last-visit risks** (SWS): flag bear points and risks added since the user last opened the Dossier; feed them into "what changed since yesterday".
6. **Personal thesis with a falsifier** (AllMind/FN2, SWS Narratives): on watchlist add or paper trade, Milo asks two questions: why you like it and what would prove you wrong; Optic then checks pillars on each data refresh and shows Holding / Weakening / Broken with evidence. No hold/sell instructions; frame as "your condition was met".
7. **Weekly portfolio digest** (SA Portfolio Digests, SWS Charlie daily review): one email per week per user covering only their names and only what changed; this is the obvious first email product once delivery exists.
8. **"Does this move matter?"** on top of commodity why-moving: pair today's explanation with the user's thesis and the score components it touched.
9. **Concentration in plain words** (Morningstar Stock Intersection, Cortex sector math): "40% of your paper book is in semiconductors."

## Unserved needs / gaps (evidence-based)

- **Teaching while showing.** Every product gives a verdict; none documented explaining concepts at the user's level in context (SA letters, Yahoo bare stats, SWS pass/fail) [DOC/OBS]. This matches Optic's known user problem (even finance majors struggle) [brief].
- **Personal, falsifiable thesis tracking for retail.** Mainstream tools offer community narratives (SWS) or rating changes (SA), not "my reasons, still true?" [MKT/DOC]. Only niche AI tools do it, with action recommendations that are risky for retail [DOC].
- **Short-term setup + long-term quality in one place, explained simply.** SWS and Morningstar ignore price action by design [DOC]; Yahoo/Robinhood explain moves without fundamentals framing [OBS/DOC]; SA has timing factors but behind $299 and without teaching [DOC/MKT].
- **Validated scores.** SWS publishes no hit rate [3P]; SA relies on self-promoted studies [3P]. A transparent "how this score has behaved historically, including misses" page would differentiate, but Optic has no validated edge yet, so it should publish honest limitations rather than performance claims [brief/INF].
- **Monthly, low-commitment pricing.** SA and SWS push annual auto-renew and draw complaints [3P]; Morningstar charges a large monthly premium [MKT]. A clear monthly plan with easy cancel is a trust lever [INF].
- **Options context for non-experts.** None of the five explains options-implied expected move in plain English on the company page [INF from all sources]; Optic already computes it.
