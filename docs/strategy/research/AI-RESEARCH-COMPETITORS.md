# AI-native research assistants: competitive research (2026-10-12)

Scope: Fiscal.ai (ex-FinChat), Robinhood Cortex, TrendSpider Sidekick, Perplexity Finance, plus Public.com Alpha and Seeking Alpha AI.
Evidence tags: DOC / MKT / OBS / 3P / INF. All sources read 2026-10-12.

Status: complete. Evidence gathered 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-13.

## 1. Fiscal.ai (formerly FinChat.io)

Sources (all read 2026-10-12): pricing https://fiscal.ai/pricing/ (OBS, loaded in browser pane), Terminal page https://fiscal.ai/products/terminal/ (OBS), MCP page https://fiscal.ai/products/mcp/ (OBS), help FAQ https://fiscal.ai/help/ (OBS/DOC), changelog https://fiscal.ai/changelog/ (OBS/DOC), user guide https://fiscal.ai/blog/ultimate-guide-to-using-fiscal-AI/ (DOC), WallStreetZen review updated 2025-07-08 https://www.wallstreetzen.com/blog/finchat-io-fiscal-ai-review/ (3P).

**Headline finding: Fiscal.ai has moved away from "chat with your stock" toward "auditable data + bring your own AI".** FinChat launched in 2023 as a chat UI over financial data; it rebranded to Fiscal.ai with a Series A in June 2025 [3P, WallStreetZen]. In October 2026 the public pricing page offers three products: Terminal, MCP ("financial data connected into your AI tools") and a data-feed API [OBS, pricing]. The Terminal product page does not mention a Copilot chatbot at all; it sells "auditable fundamentals updated within minutes" [OBS, Terminal page]. The MCP page advertises native connections into Claude and ChatGPT/Codex plus a free "Skills" catalogue of pre-built research prompts [OBS, MCP page]. The changelog shows MCP became a standalone product on 2026-07-16 and a Codex/ChatGPT plugin shipped 2026-06-02 [DOC, changelog].

1. **Audience and promise.** Fundamental investors, from individuals to hedge funds; "Institutional-grade data, 100% auditable to source" [MKT, Terminal page]. Its own guide says the platform "can also be a little overwhelming for new users" [DOC, guide]. Beginners are not the target.
2. **Compelling workflow.** Dashboard of tickers with chosen metrics and a notifications bell for news/filings, then company page with stats, business description, "Bulls Say v. Bears Say", a 30-day "What's Happening" news block, 20 years of financials, segment/KPI data (e.g. AWS revenue), and click-through to the source filing for every figure [DOC, guide; MKT, MCP page "Source to Filing"].
3. **Time to first insight.** No free tier now: a 7-day trial requires a credit card and converts unless cancelled [DOC, help FAQ]. (The older guide's "2 weeks free, no card" CTA is stale.) Earlier free plan had 10 Copilot prompts/month [3P, WallStreetZen 2025].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** Data-dense; jargon handled by formulas in hover tooltips (added v5.9.4/5.9.5, Aug 2026) and a public formula list [DOC, changelog]. No beginner mode documented [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Screener with ~200 metrics and XLSX/CSV export; fundamental charting to compare peers; technical charts (RSI/MACD/SMA/EMA) only added 2026-05-20; DCF/IRR/reverse-DCF/comps templates pre-filled per company (2026-07-29); transcript annotations; earnings calendar v2; fund-letters library (~30,000 letters) [DOC, changelog]. Portfolio: enter share counts to get allocation, industry and geographic pie charts plus weighted-average stats [DOC, guide]; brokerage sync launched 2026-05-06 [DOC, changelog]. Alerts: news/filings feed, plus price alerts for moves over 5% in a day (2026-05-13) [DOC, changelog].
6. **AI.** In-terminal AI is now mostly embedded summaries: AI summaries over filings, IR documents and Morningstar research "with citation support", custom questions on summaries, and PDF export of "AI-generated research reports" [DOC, changelog v5.6.8, v5.7.2, v5.5.5]. Previously the Copilot answered with tables, charts and per-answer citations and could build screens, DCFs and peer comps from a prompt [3P, WallStreetZen]. "Agentic Consensus Estimates" (guidance + regression + news) launched in the API 2026-10-07 [DOC, changelog]. Hallucination strategy = data quality: the MCP help article frames the main problem with AI research as data quality [DOC, help]. Data-correction requests from inside the app (2026-03-20) [DOC, changelog]. Context awareness of the user's screen/portfolio is not documented [INF].
7. **Mobile.** Desktop-first; mobile navigation redesigned Aug 2026 [DOC, changelog]; reviewer called mobile weaker [3P, WallStreetZen]. No native app found [INF].
8. **Recurring value and price.** Pro $49/mo ($39/mo yearly), Max $99/mo ($79/mo yearly); API/MCP priced separately [DOC, help FAQ]. Return reasons: earnings data within 3-7 minutes of the report for US/Canada/EU/UK, notifications feed, monthly AI news summaries [DOC, help FAQ; changelog v5.7.3]. Paywall on estimates depth ("Improved estimates paywalling ... on Pro tier", 2026-09-24) and exports for Max [DOC, changelog].
9. **Weaknesses / openings.** No free tier; overwhelming for beginners by its own admission; technicals and options are thin; AI has been demoted from the hero feature; 15-minute delayed quotes [DOC, help FAQ]. Reviewer flagged cancellation/refund complaints (small sample) [3P, search summary of reviews].

**Journeys.** J1 strong for fundamentals-literate users (business description, bulls/bears, financials linked to source) but assumes vocabulary [DOC]. J2 weak (technicals only basic) [DOC]. J3 partial: "What's Happening" news block and >5% price alerts, no documented "why is it moving" explainer [DOC/INF]. J4 decent: allocation, industry and geography pies with weighted stats, brokerage sync [DOC].

**Implication for Optic [INF].** The serious-data incumbent is conceding the chat layer to general LLMs and competing on auditability. Optic cannot win on data (unlicensed Yahoo); it can win on explanation, on beginner pacing, and on in-app actions that a generic Claude/ChatGPT plus MCP cannot do (draw on my chart, build my screen, compare to my paper book).

## 2. Robinhood Cortex

Sources (read 2026-10-12): Cortex support page https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/cortex (DOC); Cortex Digests support page https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/cortex-digests/ (DOC); Cortex Assistant Disclosure PDF, created 2026-06-16, https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/cortex_assistant_disclosure.pdf (DOC); YES/NO event newsroom 2025-12-16 https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-presents-yes-no-event (MKT); HOOD Summit 2025 newsroom https://robinhood.com/newsroom/hood-summit-2025-news/ (MKT); Agentic Trading overview https://www.robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/agentic-trading-overview/ (DOC, via search summary); Genfinity 2026-07-30 https://genfinity.io/2026/07/30/robinhood-ai-trading-agent-technical-indicators-launch/ (3P); TipRanks/The Fly on custom scans widget https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/robinhood-product-manager-introduces-custom-scans-widget-within-cortex-thefly (3P, title only).

1. **Audience and promise.** Robinhood's existing retail base; Cortex is pitched as the eventual "interface for all of Robinhood" [MKT, YES/NO newsroom]. Promise: plain-language explanation of what is moving your money, plus doing things by asking.
2. **Compelling workflow.** Open the app and the Assistant proactively shows portfolio insights, news and personalised content [DOC, disclosure §9]. Ask a question; it can analyse holdings across accounts, build a watchlist, set a push alert, or pre-fill a market/limit/stop order that you confirm on the normal ticket [DOC, disclosure §1, §6, §8]. Only one order per conversation [DOC, §8].
3. **Time to first insight.** Near zero for an existing Gold customer: the Digest is already on the stock page and in Investing; no prompting needed [DOC, Digests page]. First use requires accepting the Cortex agreement [DOC, Cortex page].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** Digests are short, fixed-format, plain-language. Portfolio Digest has three sections: market backdrop, return drivers (holdings moving your value most), top movers; plus upcoming events (earnings, macro, catalysts) and a chart of "You" versus SPY (BTC only if you hold crypto) [DOC, Digests page]. This is the clearest takeaway-first format in the set.
5. **Workflows.** Screens built from plain-English criteria; the response shows the criteria, the resulting list and the data source, and may suggest more filters [DOC, Cortex page]. On Legend (desktop) Cortex builds custom chart indicators from a description, and they sync to mobile; a scanner widget monitors the market for described conditions across stocks, ETFs, crypto and futures [MKT, HOOD Summit]. A critic calls the scanner good for discovery but not a reproducible rule-based screener [3P, search summary of tapeboard.com/blog/robinhood-legend-gaps]. Separately, "Agentic Trading" (beta, launched 2026-05-27 per press) lets Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini trade via MCP in a separate pre-funded account, with a push notification per trade and one-tap disconnect; on ~2026-07-30 the agent gained ~18 on-demand indicators with custom parameters [DOC, Agentic Trading overview; 3P, Genfinity].
6. **AI.** Context: knows your account, holdings and allocation across accounts, options chains, licensed news/research, fundamentals and analyst ratings [DOC, disclosure §5]. Retains a history of data lookups and actions and may reference them in later conversations [DOC, §9]. Web access is deliberately restricted to Robinhood-owned media (Sherwood), company IR sites and government sources; no open web, no social media [DOC, §5]. Models from third parties incl. OpenAI and Google [DOC, §9]. Guardrails: no buy/sell recommendations, no price predictions, hypotheticals labelled as such; "Responses ... not research"; Robinhood says it has no obligation to update a response that becomes outdated [DOC, §2-4]. Cannot place options/futures/multi-leg orders, though it can analyse option chains [DOC, §8]. Voice in, text out [DOC, §8]. Escalates to the support chatbot with context [DOC, §7]. Chats persist 2 hours idle, history 30 days [DOC, Cortex page].
7. **Mobile.** Assistant is iOS-only at launch; not on Android, web or Legend [DOC, §8]. Custom indicators built on Legend appear on mobile automatically [MKT, HOOD Summit] — strong cross-device continuity for charts.
8. **Recurring value and price.** Everything Cortex is behind Gold, $5/mo or $50/yr [DOC, Cortex page]. Portfolio Digests refresh several times a day or when major news breaks [MKT, YES/NO newsroom]. Push alerts set by the assistant [DOC, §8]. The paywall is cheap because AI is a retention tool for a brokerage that earns on trading and interest [INF].
9. **Weaknesses / openings.** Digests cover only the "most popular" stocks/ETFs; no options/futures digests; gaps during splits/mergers [DOC, Digests page]. Assistant iOS-only; no IRAs for Portfolio Digests [DOC]. Only your Robinhood holdings count (no outside or paper portfolios) [INF]. Explicitly "not research", so it summarises rather than teaches or argues a case [DOC/INF]. CEO conceded models "sometimes fight you" on trade instructions [3P, Genfinity]. Requires a Robinhood brokerage account [INF].

**Journeys.** J1: assistant answers questions and Digest summarises the stock, but no bull/bear or structured dossier documented [DOC/INF]. J2: custom indicators, natural-language scans, ~18 indicators via agent; no thesis framework [MKT/3P]. J3: best in class — the asset Digest exists precisely to explain what may be affecting price, sourced from news, analyst reports, technicals [DOC]. J4: strong — return drivers, top movers and allocation analysis across accounts [DOC].

**Implication for Optic [INF].** Robinhood sets the $5 price anchor and the "Digest" format (fixed three-part, chart versus SPY, upcoming events) that retail users will compare Optic's "what changed" against. Optic's edge must be teaching and the case-making Robinhood legally avoids ("not research"), plus working without a brokerage account.

## 3. TrendSpider Sidekick

Sources (read 2026-10-12): Sidekick KB article https://help.trendspider.com/kb/sidekick-ai/trendspider-sidekick (DOC); Sidekick Prompting Guide, dated 2026-05-14, https://help.trendspider.com/kb/sidekick-ai/sidekick-prompting-guide-1 (DOC); pricing https://trendspider.com/pricing/ (MKT); product page https://trendspider.com/product/sidekick-ai-trading-assistant/ (MKT, search listing only); launch blog https://trendspider.com/blog/sidekick-trading-ai/ (MKT, listing only).

1. **Audience and promise.** Active technical traders who already live in TrendSpider charts, scanners and backtests. Sidekick is an operator for the platform, not only a chat box [DOC, prompting guide].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Chart open, ask "what am I looking at?" — Sidekick literally sees the open chart including the user's indicators, drawings and annotations [DOC, KB]. Then "scan the S&P 500 for X, rank top 10", "set alerts on these", "add them to a new watchlist", "explain this backtest" (an AI Explain button in the Strategy Tester) [DOC, KB/guide].
3. **Time to first insight.** Four large customisable quick-prompt buttons when Sidekick opens [DOC, KB]. But TrendSpider itself requires a paid 14-day trial ($19-$49, non-refundable) [MKT, pricing], so a new user pays before the first insight [INF].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** No fixed response format: users request tables, rankings, verdicts or bull/bear cases; answers can include tables and line/pie/column charts [DOC]. Jargon help is contextual: right-click any indicator to have Sidekick explain it [DOC, KB]. The prompting guide teaches a four-part prompt: action, scope, filters, output format, plus "power words" (Scan, Set alerts, Add, Code, Go deep) [DOC, guide]. This shifts the burden of structure to the user [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Creates, runs, saves and schedules scans; multi-factor alerts on a ticker or whole watchlist (up to 20 tickers via "Set alerts"); edits watchlists and colour tags; codes custom JavaScript indicators; designs and optimises Strategy Tester strategies; "Go deep" runs parallel sub-agents for multi-part research [DOC, KB/guide]. Chart actions: labelled horizontal support/resistance lines, horizontal areas, vertical arrows, historical levels, options open-interest strikes and expected-move boundaries [DOC, guide]. Cannot judge trend-line quality [DOC, KB].
6. **AI.** Context: chart, named watchlists, backtest results, and read-only live broker holdings if SignalStack is connected [DOC, KB]. Data: TrendSpider feeds only (fundamentals, 10-K/20-F, transcripts, estimates, unusual options, dark pool, short volume, insider/congressional/institutional trades, seasonality); no internet; real-time candles fetched on request, not streamed [DOC, KB]. **Personas**: six selectable voices (neutral, long-term "Warren", Socratic "Graham", terse data-first, blunt sceptic "Karen" that challenges your thesis, and a meme-trader tone); chosen before a chat, not mid-chat; "all personas prioritize factual accuracy" [DOC, KB/guide]. **Root prompt**: standing instructions (style, timeframes, preferred indicators, format) applied to every message, subordinate to Sidekick rules [DOC]. Configured not to give direct trading advice; cannot place trades [DOC, KB]. Limit of ~15-20 tickers per analysis unless via scan [DOC]. Share single message or whole chat [DOC]. Uncertainty handling not documented [INF]. User picks among several frontier LLMs; top models only on higher tiers [MKT, pricing].
7. **Mobile.** Sidekick availability on TrendSpider's iOS/Android apps is not documented [DOC gap]. Chats with alert/scan actions require the browser tab to stay open [DOC, KB] — a continuity weakness.
8. **Recurring value and price.** Platform from ~$49.68/mo promo (Standard) up to $233-349/mo (Advanced) [MKT, pricing]. Sidekick sold as a separate AI add-on: free 25 messages/month on every plan; Basic $49/mo for 100; Plus $129 for 300; Max $349 for 1,000 [MKT, pricing]. A message is charged only when a text response arrives [DOC, KB]. Recurrence comes from scheduled scans and alerts Sidekick creates [DOC].
9. **Weaknesses / openings.** Expensive (AI alone can exceed the platform price); expert-oriented; prompt engineering expected of the user; no documented uncertainty or staleness handling; no beginner explanations beyond indicator right-click [MKT/DOC/INF].

**Journeys.** J1: "complete stock brief" sample prompt covers earnings trends, margins, insiders, options flow, technicals, bull and bear [DOC, guide]. J2: best in class — scan, chart levels, multi-factor alerts, backtest and AI Explain [DOC]. J3: can pull filings/news-like data and flow, but no web/news browsing documented; "why is it moving" weaker [DOC/INF]. J4: only via SignalStack-connected holdings, read-only [DOC].

**Implication for Optic [INF].** Sidekick validates Milo's direction (sees the screen, draws grounded levels, builds screens, sets alerts). Two directly adaptable ideas: right-click "explain this indicator" (Optic has "Explain this") and selectable reasoning stances, especially a "challenge my thesis" mode. Optic should not copy the four-part prompting burden; it should infer scope from page context and offer chips.

## 4. Perplexity Finance

Sources (read 2026-10-12/13): OBS — loaded without login in the browser pane: https://www.perplexity.ai/finance (markets home), https://www.perplexity.ai/finance/NVDA (overview), /NVDA/analysis, /NVDA/earnings (did not accept the cookie banner, did not sign in). WealthManagement.com 2026-03-12 https://www.wealthmanagement.com/artificial-intelligence/perplexity-upgrades-finance-capabilities (3P); search summaries of Plaid/Perplexity coverage https://plaid.com/blog/powering-intellligent-finance-with-perplexity/ and https://www.testingcatalog.com/perplexity-launches-personal-finance-powered-by-plaid/ (3P); Fintool benchmark https://fintool.com/benchmark/perplexity-versus-fintool (3P, competitor-run); Motley Fool board https://discussion.fool.com/t/perplexity-et-al/121657 (3P anecdote).

1. **Audience and promise.** Broad retail plus pros ("Computer for Finance" label in the header) [OBS]. Promise: a free, Google-Finance-like quote site where every block is an AI synthesis with source counts [OBS/INF].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Ticker page leads with a **"Notable Price Movement"** feed: one dated paragraph per trading day explaining the close (cause, magnitude in %, sector/index context, analyst actions, what investors watch next), each with a "N sources" chip (3-9 per day) [OBS, NVDA overview]. Below: Stories & Analysis links, "Key Issues", a plain-language business description, analyst consensus bar, earnings history, Polymarket prediction markets related to the company, peers [OBS]. A persistent box "Ask anything about NVIDIA Corporation" is scoped to the page; on the analysis tab it reads "...NVIDIA Corporation research", on earnings "...earnings" [OBS] — page-context scoping by tab.
3. **Time to first insight.** Seconds, no login: the "why did it move" paragraph is above the fold on a public URL [OBS]. Strongest SEO/zero-friction entry of the group [INF].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** Takeaway first, then evidence. Earnings tab: quarter strip with each quarter's post-earnings move (%), then the latest call with revenue/EPS estimate vs actual and beat %, 1-day price move, a "Listen" audio button, and tabs Highlights / Transcript / Documents; Highlights are five dense bullet paragraphs (records, guidance, margin reset, partnerships, capital returns) [OBS]. Analysis tab: consensus, analyst-by-analyst target table with upside %, "Open Web Research Reports" each labelled Outlook Bullish/Bearish/Neutral with date and "View Source", then an AI "Analyst Ratings Synthesis" that ends with a caution paragraph on near-term risks [OBS]. Jargon is not defined (Adj. EPS, GAAP margins, consensus) [OBS/INF].
5. **Workflows.** Markets home: futures/VIX strip, "Market Summary" (updated N seconds ago, 57 sources) with one lead story and headline list, S&P 500 heatmap by sector, "Discover" news clusters with source counts, watchlist, prediction markets, gainers/losers/active, sectors, crypto, fixed income [OBS]. Nav also lists Earnings, Predictions, Screener, Politicians (congressional trades), Watchlist, Portfolio, Workflows, App Gallery [OBS]. Portfolio: link brokerages via Plaid; ask about equity exposure, allocation drift and risk scenarios; insight only, "does not place trades or move funds" [3P, WealthManagement]. Bank/card/loan linking added to Computer 2026-04-09 [3P, search summary]. Tasks: scheduled briefs and stock alerts created in natural language [3P, search summary].
6. **AI.** Citations on every generated block (source counts) [OBS]. Data partners disclosed in the page footer: Financial Modeling Prep, Unusual Whales (options), Quartr (transcripts/audio), Fiscal.ai (reported revenue/EPS), S&P Global (estimates), Polymarket, TradingView charts [OBS]. Notably Fiscal.ai supplies Perplexity's earnings data [OBS] — the data vendor and AI front end are separating. Computer gets "40+ live finance tools" with SEC, FactSet, S&P, LSEG, Quartr data [3P, WealthManagement]. Guardrail is a footer disclaimer (informational, not advice) [OBS]; the analysis synthesis presents sell-side consensus without a house view [OBS]. Quality issues: competitor benchmark claims 45% accuracy and a cited-10-K figure off by $400M, and reliance on secondary sites [3P, Fintool — biased source]; a user reported an undated months-old Seeking Alpha quote used after a 45% drop [3P, Motley Fool]. Staleness handling: freshness stamps on market summary ("Updated 28 seconds ago") and "Market closed. Check back when trading resumes." [OBS].
7. **Mobile.** Perplexity apps exist; finance-specific mobile parity not verified [INF]. Plaid linking reportedly desktop-first in US/Canada [3P, search summary].
8. **Recurring value and price.** Finance pages free and public [OBS]. Pro ~$20/mo and Max ~$200/mo per third-party tables; deeper portfolio analysis and Computer tasks reportedly Pro/Max [3P, search summaries — unverified]. Perplexity claims over 75% of paying users use Finance [3P, WealthManagement]. Return reasons: daily price-move paragraphs, live market summary, earnings audio/transcripts, scheduled Tasks [OBS/3P].
9. **Weaknesses / openings.** Dense prose with no definitions; no "so what for me" unless you link accounts; consensus-reporting, not judgment; known accuracy/staleness complaints [OBS/3P]. No charting depth or drawing; no paper portfolio [INF].

**Journeys.** J1: very good fast start (description, key issues, consensus, earnings highlights) but no beginner scaffolding [OBS]. J2: weak (no technical tooling observed on the ticker page) [OBS/INF]. J3: best in class and free — dated per-day move explanations with sources, retained as a scrollable history [OBS]. J4: via Plaid Portfolio, exposure/drift/risk-scenario Q&A [3P].

**Implication for Optic [INF].** The free bar for "why did it move today" is now a sourced, dated paragraph on a public page with no login. Optic's daily Read and "what changed" must at least match that (dated, sourced, history kept) and then beat it with definitions, a beginner layer, and "what this means for your watchlist/paper book". The per-day move history on a public ticker URL is also an SEO pattern Optic lacks.

## 5. Public.com (Alpha, now "Agents")

Sources (read 2026-10-12/13): Alpha FAQ, updated 2024-05-20, https://help.public.com/en/articles/9354354-what-is-alpha (DOC, possibly stale); https://public.com/alpha which now redirects to https://public.com/ai-agents (OBS, loaded in browser pane); press release 2026-03-31 https://digital-release.wowktv.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260331NY22354/public-becomes-the-first-brokerage-to-introduce-ai-agents-for-your-portfolio (MKT, via search summary); aiwiki summary https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/public_ai_agents (3P, via search summary).

1. **Audience and promise.** Self-directed Public brokerage customers. Original Alpha (May 2023, GPT-4) was an "investing co-pilot" over filings, transcripts, quotes, analyst ratings, news and social sentiment, free to all Public investors [DOC, FAQ]. The Alpha URL now redirects to "AI Agents. For your portfolio." — Public pitches itself as an "Agentic Brokerage" [OBS].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Describe a task in plain English; the AI asks what's missing (account, size, timing), then proposes an editable Agent workflow you must explicitly approve; once active it monitors conditions and acts when criteria are met [OBS]. Or pick a ready-made Agent from a marketplace in five categories [OBS]. Examples shown range from cash sweeps to event-driven trades on Fed/CPI outcomes, with a note that some capabilities are not yet available and examples are illustrative [OBS].
3. **Time to first insight.** Alpha (legacy) was one gesture away: swipe down on any stock page in the app, hover a price chart on web, also on portfolio screen, and from an "Alpha" button on news-alert notifications [DOC, FAQ]. Requires a Public account [INF].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** Not documented in detail for current product [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Agents also do research: 30 years of earnings, financials and price data plus real time; portfolio insights from actual holdings (historical performance, current exposure); **trade planning** — walk through scenarios, potential risk and exit points [MKT, ai-agents page]. Activity feed logs every check, including when a condition was checked and not met; per-agent trade ledger [OBS]. Agents launched for select members 2026-03-31 with a waitlist; marketplace announced 2026-08-17; prediction-market agents 2026-09-24 [3P, search summaries].
6. **AI.** Portfolio-aware by design ("wired to your portfolio") [MKT]. Guardrails: outputs "informational and illustrative", user solely responsible for strategy suitability; legacy Alpha flagged as "experimental technology" that may be inaccurate [OBS; DOC, FAQ]. Unlike Robinhood, active Agents can submit transactions without per-execution approval once the workflow is approved [3P, search summary]. Citation format not documented [DOC gap].
7. **Mobile.** Alpha on app and web [DOC]; Agents reportedly on mobile and web since August [3P].
8. **Recurring value and price.** Alpha free [DOC]. Agents reportedly do not require Public Premium; standard trading fees apply [3P, search summary of support page]. Generated Assets (AI-built custom portfolios from a prompt) carry a 0.49% annual advisory fee [3P, search summary]. Recurrence = agents running continuously plus notifications [INF].
9. **Weaknesses / openings.** Automation-first; little teaching; meme-style examples (e.g. trading on presidential posts) signal a trader audience; risk of users automating ideas they don't understand [OBS/INF].

**Journeys.** J1: legacy Alpha Q&A over filings/news [DOC]. J2: "trade planning" scenarios with risk and exit [MKT]. J3: Alpha button on news alerts explains the alert [DOC]. J4: portfolio insights on exposure [MKT].

**Implication for Optic [INF].** "Plain English to a monitored rule" is becoming normal. Optic's watches are the non-brokerage, no-money-at-risk version of this; the "AI asks what's missing before building" step and the activity log that records checks that did not fire are both directly adaptable to watches.

## 6. Seeking Alpha (Virtual Analyst Report, Ask Seeking Alpha)

Sources (read 2026-10-12/13): Premium offer page https://subscriptions.seekingalpha.com/lp_premium_ai_analyst (OBS, redirected to lp_premium_14); help center: https://help.seekingalpha.com/migrated/what-is-the-virtual-analyst-report , https://help.seekingalpha.com/does-seeking-alpha-use-ai-to-generate-these-reports , https://help.seekingalpha.com/what-content-is-the-virtual-analyst-report-based-on , https://help.seekingalpha.com/what-data-sources-does-ask-sa-use (DOC, via search summaries); launch article 2025-10-13 https://seekingalpha.com/article/4829617-introducing-the-cutting-edge-research-analyst-ask-seeking-alpha (MKT, via search summary).

1. **Audience and promise.** Self-directed stock pickers who value independent (crowdsourced) analysis plus Quant ratings [MKT, offer page].
2. **Compelling workflow.** Ticker page -> Quant rating and factor grades -> Virtual Analyst Report as a one-screen snapshot -> articles. AI pitched as helping "assess stocks in seconds" [MKT].
3. **Time to first insight.** Fast for subscribers; paywalled: $4.95 one-month paid trial, then auto-renews at $299/yr [OBS, offer page].
4. **Hierarchy/jargon.** Virtual Analyst Report has a fixed five-part structure: Company Description; Competitors and Unique Value; Rating explanation; Positives Raised by Analysts; Concerns Raised by Analysts [DOC]. Fixed structure makes it scannable and comparable across stocks [INF].
5. **Workflows.** Quant ratings, screeners, Earnings Call Insights (AI), "Analyze With AI" summaries, portfolios and alerts (existing SA features) [MKT].
6. **AI.** Grounded only on Seeking Alpha's own content (Quant, analyst ratings, articles, news, SEC filings, transcripts, ETF/fund data) [DOC, Ask SA sources]. The report "does not ... perform any independent analysis" and is regenerated on a schedule so may be a few days old; written by a commercial LLM and not reviewed by editors; may contain errors [DOC]. This is the most candid staleness disclosure found. Ask Seeking Alpha (Oct 2025) handles comparisons, M&A and earnings questions; tier placement inconsistent between Premium and PRO across SA's own pages [MKT/3P]. Portfolio awareness of Ask SA not documented [DOC gap].
7. **Mobile.** Mature apps (not assessed in depth) [INF].
8. **Recurring value and price.** $299/yr Premium; return driven by articles, ratings changes, alerts, earnings insights [MKT].

## Baseline expectations

1. **Plain-language daily "what moved and why"** for each stock and for the user's own list, dated, with sources [OBS Perplexity; DOC Robinhood].
2. **Visible sources or a source count** on every AI claim, and data-vendor/freshness disclosure [OBS Perplexity; DOC Fiscal].
3. **A freshness stamp** ("updated N minutes ago", "market closed") and an explicit note when an AI summary was generated [OBS Perplexity; DOC Seeking Alpha].
4. **Both sides**: bull/bear, positives/concerns, or a closing risks paragraph [DOC Fiscal, SA; OBS Perplexity].
5. **Context awareness**: AI opened from a page knows that page; AI knows holdings when they are available [DOC Robinhood, TrendSpider; OBS Perplexity].
6. **Actions from the answer**: build a screen, add to watchlist, set an alert, with user confirmation [DOC Robinhood, TrendSpider].
7. **A clear non-advice boundary** stated once and enforced in behaviour (no buy/sell calls, no price predictions) [DOC Robinhood].
8. **Plain-English screening** that shows the criteria it used [DOC Robinhood].
9. **Earnings in one view**: estimate vs actual, beat/miss %, post-earnings move, call highlights [OBS Perplexity].
10. **Some free AI** before a paywall (Perplexity pages free; Sidekick 25 messages/month; Robinhood $5) [OBS/MKT/DOC].

## Proven ideas worth adapting

1. **Fixed digest skeleton (Robinhood).** Adapt as: "Your list today" = backdrop (one sentence on market), what moved your list most and why, upcoming events this week, chart of your watchlist/paper book vs SPY. Keep it fixed so beginners learn where to look; add a one-line "what this means" under each part. [DOC Robinhood]
2. **Per-day move history (Perplexity).** Store Optic's daily "why it moved" sentence per ticker so the Dossier shows a scrollable dated history; teaches cause and effect over time and creates public pages for SEO. Add a "jargon in this paragraph" expander. [OBS Perplexity]
3. **Page-scoped ask box (Perplexity).** Milo's input should read "Ask about NVDA's options" on the options tab, etc., with 3-4 suggested questions tuned to beginner vs experienced level. [OBS Perplexity; DOC Sidekick quick-prompt buttons]
4. **Stance selector, not personas (TrendSpider).** Offer "Explain simply / Analyst detail / Challenge my idea". The challenger mode suits Optic's no-advice line: it argues the other side of the user's thesis instead of giving a call. [DOC Sidekick personas]
5. **Standing preferences (TrendSpider root prompt).** One-time "how I invest" (horizon, experience, preferred indicators) that Milo applies everywhere; doubles as the beginner/advanced switch. [DOC Sidekick]
6. **Ask-what's-missing before building (Public).** When a user says "tell me if AMD breaks out", Milo asks the one missing parameter, shows the rule in plain words, then creates the watch on confirm. Show a log of checks that did not fire so the watch feels alive. [OBS Public]
7. **Screen transparency (Robinhood).** Every AI-built screen shows the criteria, the result list, the data source, and suggested extra filters. [DOC Robinhood]
8. **Honest staleness (Seeking Alpha, Perplexity).** Stamp every AI block with when it was generated and the data as-of time; say plainly when the market is closed. [DOC SA; OBS Perplexity]
9. **Escalation with context (Robinhood).** When Milo cannot do something, hand off to the right page with the context filled. [DOC Robinhood §7]

## Unserved needs / gaps

1. **Teaching while answering.** None of the six documents a beginner mode or inline definitions inside AI answers; Fiscal admits it overwhelms new users; Perplexity's paragraphs are jargon-dense [DOC Fiscal guide; OBS Perplexity]. Optic's audience (finance students, overwhelmed retail) is unserved here.
2. **Research without a brokerage account.** Robinhood's and Public's AI require their brokerages; Sidekick's holdings view needs SignalStack; Perplexity needs Plaid linking [DOC/MKT/3P]. Paper portfolios plus watchlists give Optic a "portfolio-aware" AI for users who will not link a broker.
3. **Case-making within a no-advice line.** Robinhood says Cortex responses are "not research"; Seeking Alpha's report does "no independent analysis"; Perplexity reports consensus [DOC/OBS]. A structured "here is the argument for, against, and what would change it" (without a call) is left open.
4. **Options explained simply.** Robinhood's assistant can't place options orders and Digests exclude options; Sidekick has flow and expected-move lines but for experts [DOC]. Plain-English options analytics (expected move, skew) is a gap Optic already has the data for.
5. **Free, friction-free depth.** Fiscal has no free tier; SA $299/yr; TrendSpider needs a paid trial; Robinhood requires Gold [DOC/OBS/MKT]. Only Perplexity is free and it is shallow on technicals and teaching [OBS].
6. **Uncertainty handling inside answers.** Disclaimers are legal boilerplate; none of the sources documents answer-level confidence or "what I don't know" sections [DOC/OBS]. Optic can show confidence and data gaps per answer (e.g. "no validated edge for this score").
7. **Accuracy trust.** Accuracy complaints about general AI finance answers (Perplexity cited-figure error, stale undated quotes) [3P Fintool — competitor-run; 3P Motley Fool]. Grounding Milo's numbers in Optic's computed values, and labelling them with source and time, can address this.
