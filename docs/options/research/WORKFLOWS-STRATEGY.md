## 1. Contract selection workflows (thesis -> timeframe -> expiry -> strike -> structure)

### 1.1 The common skeleton
Every serious source orders the decision the same way, though none publishes it as a single checklist:
1. **Directional / volatility view** (bullish, bearish, neutral; expect IV up or down). OIC organizes its whole strategy library by outlook and separately by volatility view, and its Quick Guide rates each strategy by outlook, max risk/reward, and the sign of volatility and time-erosion effects. **HEUR** (pedagogical taxonomy, not a tested rule). Src: https://www.optionseducation.org/strategies/acquire-stock ; https://prd-web.theocc.com/getmedia/f34f8a0d-806f-4f1a-adf7-d49d8d94b16e/option-strategies-quick-guide.pdf
2. **Timeframe of the thesis** -> pick an expiry that outlasts it (and know which events, e.g. earnings, ex-dividend, fall inside it).
3. **Strike / delta** -> trades off cost, leverage, and chance of finishing ITM.
4. **Structure** -> long premium (debit) vs short premium (credit), defined vs undefined risk, which is mostly a choice about the volatility view and capital.
5. **Exit plan** -> profit target, time stop, max loss.

Required data: underlying price, chain (strike, expiry, bid/ask, IV), event calendar (earnings, ex-div). **Yahoo?** chain yes; earnings date partially (yfinance `calendar`/`earnings_dates`, unreliable); ex-dividend date yes (yfinance `info`/`dividends`, next date sometimes missing). Optic already has an "entry plan that picks contracts"; the gap is mostly about labeling *why* and showing events inside the expiry.

### 1.3 The tastytrade rules: what is heuristic, what is backtested, and the limits
- **45 DTE entry. HEUR with in-house backtests.** Rationale given: theta decay steepens around there while gamma is still modest. tastylive's "30 days of theta" segment reports 45 DTE beat longer cycles in an 11-year SPY test. Src: https://tastylive.com/shows/the-skinny-on-options-abstract-applications/episodes/enter-at-45-dte-exit-at-21-dte-07-27-2020 ; https://tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/30-days-of-theta-11-14-2016
- **16 delta (approx. 1 SD) strangles / 30 delta puts. HEUR + in-house backtests.** Their varying-delta study (SPY 2005-) found higher delta -> higher average P/L but lower win rate, the usual trade-off, not an optimum. Src: https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/strangles-with-varying-deltas-11-22-2016
- **Manage at 50% of max profit / exit at 21 DTE. HEUR + in-house backtests.** tastylive reports (SPY 1 SD strangles) that exiting at 21 DTE had the highest return and lowest volatility vs holding or 50%; for SPY 16-delta short puts 2005-2017, managing at 25%, 50% or 21 DTE all beat holding to expiry. For straddles they prefer 25%. Src: https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/How-Capital-Allocation-Impacts-Volatility ; https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/short-put-management-performance-09-12-2018 ; https://tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/why-manage-at-25-not-50-for-short-straddles-09-06-2016
- **Limits of this evidence:** studies are video segments without full methodology, mostly a single underlying (SPY) over a period dominated by a bull market and a positive variance risk premium; fills often at mid; commissions rarely stated; not peer reviewed; the publisher's business is selling active trading (conflict). Independent test: FlashAlpha's SPY put-spread study found that moving from mid fills to realistic limit fills cut CAGR by 30-60% across its grid. **EMP (single practitioner study, treat as indicative).** Src: https://flashalpha.com/articles/spy-put-credit-spread-active-backtest-mm-fills-vrp-signal-drawdown-breaker
- **Why short premium "works" in these tests (where it does): EMP.** Academic evidence that selling volatility earns a premium on average: Hu, Kirilova, Park & Ryu (Management Science 2024; KOSPI 200 account data) find vol selling is the most profitable style for both retail and institutions. Index evidence: Cboe PUT index returned roughly the S&P 500 with about two-thirds of its volatility 1986-2017 (Asset Consulting Group, Cboe-linked). Caveat: these strategies have crash-concentrated losses; win rate says little about tail risk. Src: https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v70y2024i7p4742-4761.html ; https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-income-generation-and-smoother-returns-with-cboes-bxm-bxmd-put-and-cmbo-indices
- **Covered calls: EMP decomposition.** Israelov & Nielsen (FAJ 2015, AQR authors with commercial interest) find most covered-call risk and return is plain equity exposure; the short-vol piece had a Sharpe near 1.0 but only ~10% of risk; an implicit equity-reversal bet carried ~25% of risk for little return. Implication: "income" framing overstates what a covered call is. Src: https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2015/covered-calls-uncovered
- **BXM (Whaley 2002, Cboe-commissioned):** 1988-2001 BXM monthly mean 1.12% vs 1.19% S&P with 2.66% vs 4.10% st. dev.; calls written at the bid, but taxes and real fund costs not modeled. **EMP (sponsor-linked).** Src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBOE_S%26P_500_BuyWrite_Index (summarizing Whaley, J. Derivatives 2002)

### 1.4 Delta, probability ITM, probability of touch, breakeven
- **Delta as probability: MATH approximation.** Under Black-Scholes, prob(ITM at expiry, risk-neutral) = N(d2) for a call, while call delta = N(d1). They differ by sigma*sqrt(T); the gap matters for long-dated or high-IV options. Using delta is a shortcut tastytrade explicitly teaches. Src: https://www.tastylive.com/concepts-strategies/probability-of-profit
- **Probability of touch ~ 2 x prob ITM: MATH approximation** (reflection principle for driftless Brownian motion); thinkorswim shows both. Src: https://toslc.thinkorswim.com/center/howToTos/thinkManual/Analyze
- **Risk-neutral vs real-world: MATH caveat.** Probabilities from IV are risk-neutral; IV usually exceeds realized vol (variance risk premium), and real distributions are fat-tailed and skewed. So IV-based POP for short-premium trades tends to be understated in calm periods and badly wrong in crashes. IBKR describes its distribution as what options imply, not a forecast. Src: https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/general/education/probability_lab.php
- **POP is not expected value: MATH.** A 85% POP credit spread can have negative expectancy once costs are included; FlashAlpha's SPY matrix found average theoretical EV slightly negative and realized P/L positive only because of the period's drift and VRP. Src: https://flashalpha.com/articles/spy-put-credit-spread-matrix-8-year-backtest-theoretical-vs-realized

**Optic application (section 1).** Rationale: the thesis->expiry->strike->structure order is universal; Optic's entry plan should expose each step as a visible choice with its reason. Data: all chain fields needed are in Yahoo; events partly. Conditions: works for liquid single names/ETFs; fails on thin chains (stale `lastPrice`, zero bid). Weaknesses: delta/POP depend on Yahoo IV, which is noisy for illiquid strikes and sometimes 0 or absurd. Cost: S-M (relabel + event flags + spread %). Validation: recompute IV from mid with Optic's own BS solver and compare to Yahoo IV; for POP, track calibration (Brier score) of "prob ITM at expiry" against realized outcomes over months of saved snapshots.

## 2. Strategy analysis

### 2.1 What every strategy view must show (MATH unless noted)
- **Expiration payoff** (piecewise linear, exact) **and theoretical value before expiry ("T+0" or "today" curve, plus intermediate dates).** thinkorswim draws both by default (expiry curve and a today curve priced off IV; date/IV adjustable). Src: https://toslc.thinkorswim.com/center/howToTos/thinkManual/Analyze . The T+0 curve is model output: it needs a pricing model, per-leg IV, rate, dividends. **Calendars and diagonals have no meaningful single expiry payoff** (the long leg is still alive at the short leg's expiry) so they must be shown at the front expiry using a model value for the back leg, and the result depends heavily on the assumed back-month IV. Any tool that shows a calendar's "max profit" as a fixed number is presenting a model assumption.
- **Breakevens** at expiry (exact) and at the chosen date (model).
- **Max profit / max loss** (exact for defined-risk; "unlimited" or "to zero of the stock" for naked calls / short puts / covered calls).
- **Net debit/credit at realistic prices:** credits at the bid, debits at the ask, or mid minus a stated slippage. FINRA Rule 2220(d)(3)(D) requires options projections in member communications to disclose and reflect all relevant costs (commissions, fees, interest). Src: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2220 . ORATS backtests default to paying 66% of the spread on two-leg trades. Src: https://blog.orats.com/the-tale-of-two-backtests-why-arent-opposite-strategies-absolute-returns-equal
- **Aggregate Greeks** (sum of leg Greeks x quantity x multiplier, sign by side), shown in dollar terms (see section 4).
- **Scenario grid: price x IV x date.** Rows = underlying moves (e.g. -15%..+15%), columns = dates (today, mid, expiry), with an IV-shift toggle (e.g. -10/0/+10 vol points, or "post-earnings crush" preset). Portfolio-margin engines do exactly this: OCC TIMS revalues positions at ten equidistant points across a stress range, and Schwab describes both sticky-strike and sticky-delta IV handling. Src: https://prd-web.theocc.com/getmedia/cc2a75c2-294e-4667-a94f-7470ddc78fe8/RBH-CPM_UserGuide.pdf ; https://international.schwab.com/story/option-traders-how-portfolio-margin-works
- **Assignment / exercise risk on short legs:** American-style short calls are most at risk when ITM before an ex-dividend date and their extrinsic value is below the dividend; short puts deep ITM with little extrinsic value (high rates make this likelier). Assignment of one leg of a spread breaks the defined-risk shape. At expiry, OCC auto-exercises options ITM by $0.01 or more; holders can still give exercise instructions after the 4:00 pm close (exchange cutoff 5:30 pm ET), so a short leg near the strike (pin risk) can be assigned on after-hours moves. **MATH/rule.** Src: https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/risks-options-assignment ; https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/options/understanding-special-exercises-and-pin-risk/ ; https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/iron-condor-pin-risk-naked-short-expiration . FINRA's 2022 sweep update asks firms that show a "maximum loss" at order entry to caveat that it may not account for dividend risk. Src: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/sweep-letters-and-updates/update-option-account-opening-and-supervision
- **Margin / buying power vs max loss** (2.2).

### 2.2 Margin basics (rule text; brokers may set higher house requirements)
FINRA Rule 4210(f)(2) (strategy-based, i.e. Reg T-era customer margin). Src: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210 (read 2026-10-13); Cboe Margin Manual https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/membership/Margin_Manual.pdf (text not extractable by my tools; cited for structure only).
- **Long options:** pay in full (listed options with >9 months may be margined at 75% of value at many brokers; not verified in this read, treat as broker-specific).
- **Short uncovered equity call/put:** 100% of option market value + 20% of underlying value, minus out-of-the-money amount, floored at option value + 10% of underlying (calls) or + 10% of aggregate strike (puts). Broad-based index options use 15% instead of 20% (IBKR table). **Rule.**
- **Cash-secured put (cash account):** cash equal to the aggregate strike (net of premium at most brokers). **Broker practice.**
- **Covered call:** no margin on the call when the stock is held; the stock itself is margined or paid. **Rule** ((f)(2)(F)(ii)).
- **Spreads (verticals, iron condors, butterflies):** long legs paid in full; requirement on shorts = lesser of naked requirement or **maximum potential loss**, computed by netting intrinsic values at every strike in the spread. For a vertical credit spread this equals width minus credit; for an iron condor brokers generally charge only the wider side, since both sides cannot finish ITM together. **Rule + broker practice.** Src: https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/ja/node/600 ; Cboe circular on condor structures https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/regulation/circulars/regulatory/RG07-043.pdf
- **Short straddle/strangle:** greater of the two naked requirements plus the other side's premium (standard broker formula; verify per broker).
- **Portfolio margin (4210(g)):** requirement = largest theoretical loss across ten equidistant valuation points over a move range of +6%/-8% for high-cap broad-based indexes, +/-10% other broad-based indexes, +/-15% for single equities and narrow indexes, with a floor of $0.375 x multiplier per contract. Minimum account equity is commonly cited as $100,000 (FINRA PM FAQ; not located in my read of (g), verify). **Rule.**
- **Implication:** margin is not max loss. For undefined-risk trades the margin requirement is far below the possible loss; for defined-risk spreads they coincide. Optic must never present "buying power" or "capital required" as "risk."

### 2.3 Per-strategy checklist (what must be shown correctly)
| Strategy | Must show | Common display error |
|---|---|---|
| Long call / put | Debit at ask; breakeven = K +/- premium; max loss = premium; prob. finishing past breakeven (not just ITM); theta per day in $; IV crush if earnings inside expiry | "Prob ITM" shown as if it were chance of profit |
| Vertical (debit/credit) | Width, net price, max P/L, breakeven = short strike -/+ credit (credit spreads) or long strike +/- debit; prob. max loss; early-assignment on short leg; pin risk between strikes | Credit at mid; ignoring that max loss occurs with probability, not just "unlikely" |
| Covered call | Combined payoff incl. stock; upside cap; downside = stock minus premium; ex-div assignment; tax/called-away note | Framing premium as "income" or "yield" without the stock risk (Israelov & Nielsen) |
| Cash-secured put | Cash reserved = strike x 100; effective purchase price = K - premium; downside to zero; assignment any time (American) | Annualized "return on capital" headlines (Rule 2220(d)(3)(H) requires formulas and 60-day experience for annualized figures in member comms) |
| Calendar / diagonal | Value at front expiry as a model curve; sensitivity to back-month IV and to term-structure shift; vega positive; ex-div on short leg | Showing fixed max profit; using one IV for both expiries |
| Straddle / strangle (long) | Breakevens both sides; required move vs implied move vs historical earnings moves; post-event IV crush scenario | Treating the implied move as a forecast range |
| Straddle / strangle (short) | Undefined loss; margin vs stress loss (-15%/+X vol); gamma near expiry; assignment | "High POP" without tail scenario |
| Iron condor | Four legs at realistic fills; both breakevens; max loss = wider wing - credit; prob. of max loss on each side; skew effect on placement | Mid fills on four legs (spread cost can exceed a large share of the credit) |

**Optic application (section 2).** Rationale: these are the minimum correct displays; Optic's strategy ideas currently cover verticals and single legs. Data: chain + rate + dividends, all approximately available (rate: use 13-week T-bill `^IRX` from Yahoo; dividends from yfinance). Failure modes: Yahoo IV per strike is noisy; T+0 curves inherit that noise; American early exercise not captured by BS (use a binomial/BAW model or flag). Cost: M for T+0 + scenario grid; L for calendars with term-structure modeling. Validation: price-reconstruction test (model value of each leg at today's IV should land within the bid-ask); unit tests against textbook payoff values; compare T+0 curve to OptionStrat/thinkorswim for a few fixed snapshots.

## 3. Presentation for mixed audiences and regulation

### 3.1 How tools avoid overwhelming beginners (HEUR, design practice)
- **Lead with the risk graph and three numbers** (max loss, max profit, breakeven), Greeks behind a toggle: thinkorswim Risk Profile, OptionStrat builder, tastytrade "curve" page (which also shows the expected move). Src: https://tastylive.com/news-insights/options-earnings ; https://optionstrat.com/blog/options-profit-calculator-tutorial
- **Outlook-first navigation** (OIC strategy pages by bullish/bearish/neutral/volatility). Src: https://www.optionseducation.org/strategies/acquire-stock
- **Pre-filter illiquid contracts** (OptionStrat removes low OI/volume and <= $0.05 premium). Src: https://optionstrat.com/blog/introducing-options-optimizer
- **Tiered access.** Brokers gate strategies by approval level (covered calls/protective puts -> long options -> spreads -> naked writing); the tiers are firm practice under Rule 2360, not a rule-defined scale. FINRA's sweep asks firms to tailor promotional material to experience level. Src: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/sweep-letters-and-updates/update-option-account-opening-and-supervision . Optic's "advanced switch = knowledge ladder" fits this pattern.
- **Event warnings.** I found no documentation that thinkorswim or tastytrade flags, at the expiry level in the chain, that earnings fall inside the expiry; thinkorswim shows event icons on charts and positions (within eight weeks), tastytrade shows earnings dates on the watchlist. Data vendors (Wall Street Horizon) sell flags for earnings-date changes that cross an expiry. Src: https://tickertape.tdameritrade.com/tools/thinkorswim-earnings-feature-15768 ; https://wallstreethorizon.com/news/options-intelligence . This is a gap Optic can fill cheaply.

### 3.2 Regulatory baseline (US). Optic is a research publisher, not a broker-dealer; these rules bind FINRA members, but they are the best public statement of what "fair" options presentation looks like, and any broker partner (e.g. Optic's planned SnapTrade link) will judge Optic by them. Not legal advice.
- **FINRA Rule 2360 + OCC ODD.** Brokers must deliver the OCC "Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options" at or before account approval and do diligence on objectives, experience, finances. The current ODD version dates from June 3, 2024; OCC now updates the document instead of issuing supplements. **Rule.** Src: https://www.theocc.com/Company-Information/Documents-and-Archives/Options-Disclosure-Document ; https://innreg.com/resources/finra-rules/2360-options
- **FINRA Rule 2220 (options communications).** Before ODD delivery, retail options communications are limited to general descriptions, must say where to get the ODD, and may not include past or projected performance or name specific securities ((d)(1)(A)). Projections (incl. annualized returns) are allowed only if the ODD precedes them, no certainty is implied, parameters are clearly defined (strikes, prices, premium, dividends), all costs reflected, assumptions stated, risks disclosed; annualized returns need 60 days of experience, shown formulas, and a statement that they require the parameters to recur ((d)(3)). Historical records need a defined universe, at least 12 months, costs, and ROP sign-off ((d)(4)). **Rule.** Src: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2220
- **Probability-of-profit numbers.** No FINRA rule names "POP." The closest standards are 2220(d)(3) (projection conditions) and Rule 2210's general ban on predicting or projecting performance; a 2026 FINRA proposal to relax 2210 for projections (SR-FINRA-2026-004, filed Feb 2026, amended Jun 2026, still pending as far as I found) keeps 2220's options provisions and notes that calculators predicting the likelihood of outcomes are outside an existing exception. **Rule (pending).** Src: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/SR-FINRA-2026-004-Federal-Register-Notice.pdf ; https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SR-FINRA-2026-004-Response-to-Comments-20260630.pdf . Practical reading (HEUR): show POP as a model output, name the model and the IV used, state that it ignores early management, costs, and fat tails, and never as a headline.
- **FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-08** (Mar 2022): concern about retail self-directed access to options and complex products; reminder of Rules 2360/2220 and Reg BI; asked for comment on effective practices. Src: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/Regulatory-Notice-22-08.pdf
- **Gamification.** SEC 2021 request for comment on digital engagement practices led to the 2023 predictive-data-analytics proposal, which the SEC withdrew on June 12, 2025. Massachusetts settled with Robinhood in Jan 2024 ($7.5M): stop celebratory animations tied to trading frequency, game-of-chance features, and certain push notifications; add disclosures to curated lists. **Rule/enforcement.** Src: https://www.psca.org/news/psca-news/2025/6/sec-withdraws-14-gensler-era-proposals/ ; https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/robinhood-pays-75m-settle-massachusetts-gamification-charge . Design implication: no celebrations, streaks, or "hot contract" push alerts in Optic's options features; the Scout mascot should never cheer an options trade.
- **Publisher status (flag for counsel).** The SEC's investment-adviser "publisher" exclusion (Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181, 1985) covers impersonal, bona fide, regularly published content. An "entry plan that picks contracts" for a user, or personalization from a linked brokerage account, moves toward individualized advice. **Legal question, not a finding.** Src: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/472/181/

### 3.3 Minimum caveats for any probability claim (derived from 2220(d)(3) + MATH)
1. Model named (e.g. "lognormal, using each strike's implied volatility as of [time], delayed ~15 min").
2. Risk-neutral, not a forecast; real moves have fatter tails and IV usually exceeds realized vol.
3. Measured at expiry only; ignores early exits, early assignment, dividends unless stated.
4. Computed at stated fill prices (bid/ask, not mid) and including commissions if any.
5. Event risk: says whether an earnings date falls inside the expiry and that the model may not price the jump separately.
6. Paired with max loss and probability of max loss, never alone.

## 4. Portfolio-level options risk

- **Dollar Greeks (MATH):** dollar delta = delta x multiplier x S x qty (P/L per 100% move; per 1% move divide by 100); dollar gamma per 1% move = 0.5 x gamma x (0.01 S)^2 x multiplier x qty; theta per day = theta x multiplier x qty; vega per vol point = vega x multiplier x qty.
- **Beta-weighted delta (HEUR built on MATH):** converts each position to SPY-equivalent shares: BWD_i = delta_i x multiplier x qty x beta_i x S_i / S_SPY. tastylive example: -31 SPY-weighted delta means about -$31 per +$1 SPY move. thinkorswim offers it as a checkbox in Activity and Positions. Choice of benchmark matters (SPX/SPY for large caps, RUT for small caps); low correlation makes the number misleading. Src: https://toslc.thinkorswim.com/center/howToTos/thinkManual/Monitor/Activity-and-Positions/Beta-Weighting ; https://tastylive.com/shows/tasty-bites/episodes/the-importance-of-beta-weighted-deltas-01-12-2016 . Weakness: beta is estimated (window, frequency), unstable, and assumes a linear one-factor relationship; it understates idiosyncratic events (earnings) and correlation spikes in selloffs.
- **Stress scenarios (MATH + rule-inspired):** full revaluation of every leg on a grid like the PM ranges (-15%..+15% single names, -8%..+6% index) with vol shifts (e.g. +10 vol points on down moves, -5 on up moves, i.e. spot-vol correlation), plus a "gap" scenario (earnings: +/- 1.5x implied move with IV collapsing to post-event levels). Schwab notes the worst array across sticky-strike and sticky-delta assumptions is what PM uses. Src: https://international.schwab.com/story/option-traders-how-portfolio-margin-works
- **Concentration (HEUR):** share of total stress loss from the largest underlying; count of positions with earnings inside expiry; short-option notional vs account equity.
- **How brokers present it:** a positions table with per-position and total beta-weighted delta, gamma, theta, vega; a portfolio risk graph (P/L vs SPY move) using beta-scaled moves. Src as above.
- **Optic application.** Data: Optic needs user positions (manual entry or the SnapTrade read-only link, not yet shipped), betas (Optic can estimate from Yahoo daily prices: 1y daily regression vs SPY), and chains for each held contract. Cost: M once positions exist. Validation: compare Optic's beta-weighted delta and scenario P/L against a broker's figures for the same test portfolio; reconcile sum-of-legs vs full revaluation at small moves (should agree to within gamma error).

**Implications for a research tool (inferred from the above; HEUR for design, EMP for the underlying facts):**
1. **Costs are a first-order outcome, not a footnote.** Spread as % of mid should sit next to every contract and be charged in every P/L illustration (round trip). Retail-favored contracts carry ~12% spreads.
2. **Earnings are the single biggest retail trap.** Any contract whose expiry spans an earnings date needs a visible flag, the implied move vs historical move, and an explicit note that IV usually drops after the event (long premium can lose even when direction is right).
3. **Short-dated, cheap OTM long options are where losses concentrate.** Default suggestions should not steer to weeklies/0DTE or low-delta lottery tickets; if shown, show the chance of finishing ITM and spread cost plainly.
4. **Selling premium has the better average outcome in the literature, but with tail risk.** A tool can say this, but must pair any "win rate" with max loss, a stress scenario, and assignment/margin implications.
5. **Exercise/dividend mistakes are real** (Bryzgalova et al.). Flag short calls ITM before ex-dividend and long ITM calls whose time value is below the coming dividend.

## What Optic must NOT claim
1. **"X% probability of profit" as a prediction.** It is a risk-neutral model output that depends on IV choice and ignores costs and fat tails (IBKR, tastylive POP definitions). Rule 2220(d)(3) would require defined parameters, costs, assumptions, and risks for any projection.
2. **"High win rate = good strategy."** Win rate and expectancy diverge; short premium wins often and loses large (tastylive varying-delta study; FlashAlpha EV vs realized).
3. **"The 45 DTE / 16 delta / 50% / 21 DTE rules are proven."** They are heuristics supported by a broker-media firm's own SPY studies, without published costs or full methods.
4. **"Covered calls / cash-secured puts generate income/yield"** without stating that the risk is mostly the stock's (Israelov & Nielsen 2015); and no annualized "return on capital" headlines (2220(d)(3)(H) conditions).
5. **"Buying power / margin = your risk."** Margin understates loss on undefined-risk trades (4210).
6. **"Defined risk" for spreads without exceptions:** early assignment, pin risk, and dividend risk can break the shape (FINRA sweep caveat; Schwab pin-risk note).
7. **"Implied move = where the stock will trade."** It is a market-priced expectation of absolute move, and pre-earnings options are on average overpriced to retail buyers (de Silva et al.).
8. **"Unusual activity" or "smart money" as evidence of informed buying.** Optic has no trade side, no sizes, delayed quotes, and prior-day OI; retail is a large share of volume (B-P-S: >60%).
9. **"Retail traders like you profit with this."** Evidence across US, Netherlands, Korea shows retail option traders lose on average, especially buying short-dated, OTM, and pre-earnings options (B-P-S 2023; de Silva et al.; Beckmeyer et al.; Bauer et al. 2009; Hu et al. 2024). Cboe's more favorable 0DTE framing is exchange-published and has no P/L.
10. **Real-time or exact Greeks/probabilities.** Inputs are delayed ~15 min and Yahoo IV is noisy; label "as of" and "estimated."

## Formulas and definitions to adopt (units stated)
Notation: S spot ($), K strike ($), T years to expiry (calendar days/365), r continuously compounded rate (from ^IRX), q dividend yield (or discrete dividends), sigma implied vol (annualized decimal), m multiplier (100), N() standard normal CDF.
- d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r - q + sigma^2/2)T] / (sigma sqrt T); d2 = d1 - sigma sqrt T. **MATH (BSM).**
- **Prob ITM at expiry (risk-neutral):** call N(d2), put N(-d2). Do not use delta (N(d1)) except as a labeled shortcut.
- **Prob of touch before expiry (approx.):** ~ 2 x prob ITM (driftless reflection approximation; capped at 1). Label "approximate."
- **POP (expiry):** P(payoff at expiry - net cost - fees > 0) = sum of risk-neutral probability mass over price intervals where expiry P/L > 0, using lognormal with one stated sigma (ATM or breakeven-strike IV, stated). Better (later): integrate the skew-implied density (Breeden-Litzenberger from the smoothed IV curve).
- **Breakevens:** long call K + debit; long put K - debit; put credit spread K_short - credit; call credit spread K_short + credit; debit call spread K_long + debit; iron condor K_short_put - credit and K_short_call + credit. All prices per share, from the fill convention used.
- **Max loss:** vertical credit = (width - credit) x m; vertical debit = debit x m; iron condor = (max(width_put, width_call) - credit) x m; long option = premium x m; CSP = (K - premium) x m; covered call = (S_paid - premium) x m.
- **Reg T estimate, short naked equity put:** [P + max(0.20 S - max(S - K, 0), 0.10 K)] x m (call: [C + max(0.20 S - max(K - S, 0), 0.10 S)] x m); index options 15%. Label "typical minimum; your broker may require more."
- **Spread cost:** (ask - bid) / mid, %; round-trip cost estimate = (ask - bid) x m per contract per leg if crossing both ways.
- **Implied move (to expiry):** ATM straddle mid / S (%, approx. expected absolute move); 1-SD move ~ S x sigma_ATM x sqrt T (~1.25 x straddle, since E|Z| = 0.798). Earnings implied move should use the first expiry after the event, ideally net of normal-day variance: sigma_event^2 ~ sigma_front^2 T_front - sigma_base^2 (T_front - 1/252). **MATH (approximations).**
- **Extrinsic value:** option mid - max(0, intrinsic). Early-exercise flag for short call: extrinsic < next dividend and ex-date before expiry.
- **Dollar Greeks:** delta$ = delta x m x qty x S (per 1.00 change in S, $ per $ = delta x m x qty); gamma$ per 1% = 0.5 x gamma x (0.01 S)^2 x m x qty; theta$ per calendar day = theta_annual/365 x m x qty; vega$ per vol point = vega(per 1.00 vol)/100 x m x qty.
- **Beta-weighted delta (SPY shares):** sum_i delta_i x m x qty_i x beta_i x S_i / S_SPY; beta from 1y daily log returns vs SPY (state window).
- **DTE:** calendar days to expiry; flag weekly/0DTE separately.
