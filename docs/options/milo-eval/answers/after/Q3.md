The big one is earnings: $AAPL reports on Nov 2, inside this Nov 13 expiry, and your iron condor collects **$399.5** (at the mid) for betting on a quiet stretch. An iron condor is a bet that nothing happens, and earnings season is the market's way of disagreeing.

**Where it breaks.** Breakevens at expiry are **$311** and **$364** [[src options_research]], about 7.6% below and 8.1% above the $336.64 close. The options market prices a 6.39% move to this expiry and a one-standard-deviation move of 8.02%, so the breakevens sit near that line and the short strikes (315 and 360, about 6.4% and 6.9% away) sit right on the straddle. The model says a one-standard-deviation move is exceeded about a third of the time. The payoff is lopsided too: max profit $399.5 against a max loss of **$1,100.5**, about 2.75 times as much (1,100.5 / 399.5).

**How it moves.** Net gamma is -0.924, so losses speed up as price moves, and vega is -24.3, so a 10-point rise in implied volatility (the price of movement) costs $208.12 now with the stock flat. The model's gamma flip is 319.33 [[src gex]], just above your 315 short put. Below it, dealer hedging would add to moves rather than damp them, though that dealer positioning is an assumption and weaker for single stocks.

**What it costs to trade.** Three of the four legs are graded Wide (the 300 put, 360 call and 375 call), with gaps of 20.2%, 11.6% and 13.5% of their mid. At the natural prices (bid for what you sell, ask for what you buy) the credit is $347.0, not $399.5, and a full round trip costs about $105, roughly 26% of the credit. Last trades are 83 hours old and the chain is Friday's, so quotes may have moved.

**Smaller ones.** The options are American style, and the model does not cover early assignment of a short leg. Max loss is not your broker's margin requirement. Optic has no implied-volatility history yet, so it cannot say whether volatility is high against its own year.
