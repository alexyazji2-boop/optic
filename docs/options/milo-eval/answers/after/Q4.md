I'm reading this as the gamma exposure (GEX) chart, which shows where options dealers would have to trade the most as $AAPL moves. Think of it as shock absorbers on a car: it tells you how bumpy the ride may be, not which way the road goes.

Each bar is a price level. Bars above zero are calls, bars below zero are puts. The tall bar is at **340**, about 1% above the $336.64 close, with **$478.4m** from calls against **-$30.7m** from puts [[src gex]]. Optic calls that the call wall.

Added up, the chart is positive: **$724.299m** per 1% move [[src gex]]. In the model, that means dealers' hedging sells rises and buys dips, which tends to calm things. For you, that means big swings are less likely, but it says nothing about direction.

The line to know is the flip point at **319.33** [[src gex]], about 5.1% below. Under it, the model says hedging would add to moves instead.

Two cautions. This assumes dealers hold the calls customers sold and the puts customers bought, and that is a guess, not observed, and it is weaker for single stocks. Also, 74.5% of the total [[src gex]] sits in options expiring Friday, Oct 16, so the picture will look different after that.
