Three costs stack up here: the gap between buy and sell prices, volatility that tends to drain after earnings, and a breakeven above where $AAPL trades now. A spread is the thrifty cousin of a plain call, but the toll booths still charge.

**Getting in and out.** The spread buys the 340 call and sells the 355 call, both expiring Nov 13. At the middle price it costs **$547.5**, but buying at the ask and selling at the bid (the prices you can actually trade at) costs **$580.0** [[src options_research]], so $32.5 more. The 355 call is the weak leg: its bid-ask gap (the distance between what buyers offer and sellers ask) is 9.6% of its price. Both legs together cost about **$65** to open and close, roughly 12% of the $547.5 you put up (65 ÷ 547.5). The quotes also come from Friday's session, with last trades 83 hours old, so they may have moved.

**Volatility and time.** Apple reports on Nov 2, before expiry. Implied volatility (how much movement the options are priced for) includes that report and usually falls after it. This position gains if it rises, so a fall hurts: in Optic's model, 16 days out with the stock unchanged, it is down $95.01 if volatility holds and $228.51 if it drops 10 points. Time decay adds another $3.96 a day.

**The finish line.** Breakeven at expiry is **$345.48**, $8.84 above the $336.64 close. A 2% rise still leaves it down $210.22 at expiry in the model.

**What argues against it:** the cost buys a capped loss of $547.5, and the spread is cheaper than the 340 call alone, which asks $9.75 a share ($975 a contract). Still just the data, not advice.
