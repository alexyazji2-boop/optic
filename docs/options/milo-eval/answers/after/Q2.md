**Reading it.** Those breakevens are for holding to expiry [[src options_research]]. Selling at two weeks keeps some time value, so they overstate the move you need, but they rank the two fairly. Delta (a rough guide to how the option's price follows the stock) says about $0.48 a share per $1 rise against $0.34. At today's decay rate, two weeks costs about $259 on the 340 (27% of its $975 ask) and $227 on the 350 (39% of its $580 ask), and decay speeds up as expiry nears. The cheaper contract gives up a bigger share to the clock.

**Costs and setting.** Getting in and out costs about $25 a contract on the 340 and $40 on the 350, and the chain is a Friday snapshot, so quotes may have moved. Implied volatility (what the options charge for movement) is 27.07% and 26.42%, against 16.5% realised over 20 days [[src technicals]]. Earnings on Nov 2 land after a two-week window but before expiry, so the price already carries them. The 340 strike is the call wall, where the model's dealer gamma peaks [[src gex]], and 344.18 resistance sits between spot and both breakevens.

**What argues against both.** Optic's own two-week expected move is 6.21% [[src technicals]], and positive dealer gamma (a model assumption) leans against big moves. CPI on Oct 14, PPI on Oct 15 and the Oct 16 expiry fall inside the window. A close under the 50-day EMA at 326.42 would undo the trend read.
