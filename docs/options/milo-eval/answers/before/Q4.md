I'm assuming you mean the gamma exposure chart by strike. It shows where market makers (the firms that sell options to everyone else) would have to buy or sell stock to protect themselves, assuming they sit on the other side of customers' trades. That is a model guess, not something anyone can see.

Across all strikes the total is **+$797.053m per 1% move** [[src gex]]. Positive means their hedging tends to sell when the stock rises and buy when it falls, a bit like shock absorbers. Calls add to the total and puts take away, and calls win here.

The tallest bar is **$340**: $578.1m, the call wall, where the most contracts are open (133,786). Under the model, rallies into it tend to stall. Below, the flip point is **323.8945**, 3.786% under 336.64. Under that level the shock absorbers stop cushioning and start adding to moves. For a holder it shows where price may feel sticky or loose, not where it is going.

Most of this comes from options that expire Friday, 76.4% of chain gamma [[src greeks]], so the picture changes next week. The chart is built off 336.64, while the daily chart shows a 340.97 close [[src technicals]], so the wall may be closer than drawn.
