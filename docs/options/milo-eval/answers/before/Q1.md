Two legs means two toll booths, and the second one costs more than the first.

The spread on this page buys the Nov 13 $335 call and sells the $355 call. At the midpoint (halfway between what buyers bid and sellers ask) that costs **$8.10 per share, or $810 for one** [[src strategy_ideas]]. The catch is that you rarely get filled at the midpoint. The 335 call is quoted 12.0 bid and 12.5 ask, a gap of 4.08%. The 355 call is 3.95 bid and 4.35 ask, a gap of **9.64%**. If you had to buy at the ask and sell at the bid, the cost would be 12.5 minus 3.95, or 8.55. That is 0.45 more than 8.10, about 5.6%, or $45 a spread before the stock has moved at all.

Thin trading makes it harder. Open interest (contracts already open) is only 177 on the long leg and 349 on the short leg, so a wide quote has little reason to tighten. The price itself is also rich: implied volatility is 1.54 times realized [[src cases]], and earnings on Nov 2 fall inside this expiry. Why it matters to you: a worse fill pushes the breakeven up from 343.1 at the midpoint to 343.55 at the worst fill (335 plus 8.55). The data has no record of what spreads like this actually fill at, so the 8.55 is a worst case, not a prediction. Still just the data, not advice.
